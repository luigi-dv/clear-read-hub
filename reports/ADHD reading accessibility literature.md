# Structure Beats Typography for ADHD Readers

The scientific literature says ADHD impairs document reading not at the level of decoding words but at the level of building and retaining a connected model of the text: working memory deficits of medium size (**d ≈ 0.4–1.0 in children, ≈ 0.5 in adults**) and lapses of sustained attention, expressed moment-to-moment as mind-wandering, are the best-supported mechanisms, losses fall disproportionately on **central ideas**, and adults with ADHD read more slowly with markedly more re-reading while standardized multiple-choice tests of short passages often show no deficit at all. ADHD-specific controlled evidence on *presentation* is extremely thin: text-to-speech is the only presentation aid with two independent ADHD-relevant comprehension studies, metacognitive scaffolding eliminated the ADHD comprehension gap in one 210-adult study, RSVP helped in one small study, keyword highlighting rests on three children, and everything else that reading apps market to ADHD users (chunking, headings, spacing, fonts, reading rulers, reader mode, dark mode, simplification) is either borrowed from dyslexia or general-reader research or has outright null results (Bionic Reading, OpenDyslexic and Dyslexie, coloured overlays, Sans Forgetica). HCI and accessibility venues have produced only two 2026 reading-interface studies with ADHD participants (21 readers of whom 6 had ADHD; 10 users with no comparison condition), the W3C COGA guidance is expert consensus rather than trial evidence, and no readability corpus carries judgements from ADHD readers. The practical implication for Clear Read Hub is that a preregistered, within-subject study of **24–36 ASRS-screened adults** reading matched long passages under a few presentation conditions, with comprehension of central ideas and probe-caught mind-wandering as primary outcomes, reviewed by a commercial or partner IRB, and submitted to ASSETS or TACCESS with a visible generative-AI disclosure, would fill a genuine gap and exceed the field's median sample of 13. One caveat governs everything below: the research session could not open full texts at ACM, Springer, SAGE, Elsevier or PMC, so many DOIs and sample sizes are flagged as unverified and must be checked before citation; every tag from the notes is preserved inline and in the grouped reference list at the end.

## Working memory and sustained attention, not decoding, drive the ADHD comprehension gap

The trait-level mechanism with the strongest meta-analytic support is working memory. Martinussen and colleagues' meta-analysis of 26 child studies reported **spatial storage d = 0.85, spatial central executive d = 1.06, verbal storage d = 0.47 and verbal central executive d = 0.43**, with deficits persisting after controlling for language and IQ ([Martinussen et al. 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15782085/)) [DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1097/01.chi.0000153228.72591.73]. Willcutt's 83-study review put pooled working-memory d at **0.55 (95% CI 0.44–0.65)** and stressed that executive deficits are medium-sized and absent in many individuals with ADHD ([Willcutt et al. 2005](https://vivo.colorado.edu/display/pubid_52773); effect size via [PMC4264104, table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4264104/table/T4)) [DOI verified: 10.1016/j.biopsych.2005.02.006]. In adults the deficit persists at moderate size: a 38-study meta-analysis gives verbal WM ES ≈ 0.55 and visuospatial ≈ 0.49, though those figures come from a secondary citation ([Alderson et al. 2013](https://vufind.lboro.ac.uk/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_miscellaneous_1353987286)) [DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1037/a0032371; effect-size figures from a secondary citation], and a review of meta-analyses places working memory at the top of the ADHD neurocognitive profile with a mean standardized difference of **0.54** versus 0.35 for set shifting ([Pievsky & McGrath 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29106438/)) [DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1093/arclin/acx055]. The frequently cited child moderator analysis by Kasper, Alderson and Hudec (2012) could not be confirmed by any search result and is listed as [unverified in this session]. The medium effect sizes matter for product design: group distributions overlap substantially, so not every ADHD reader is affected and any "ADHD preset" will misfit many users.

Two experiments tie working memory directly to document comprehension. Miller and colleagues had children with ADHD and word-reading-matched controls retell a passage scored on 47 information units; the ADHD group's largest deficit was on *central* information, the "centrality deficit", and **working memory accounted for the most unique variance** in central recall after controlling word reading ([Miller et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3561476)) [DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1007/s10802-012-9686-8]. Kofler's dual-task study of 78 children aged 8–13 showed that adding concurrent working-memory demands reduced comprehension in both groups but **more in ADHD**, robust to controls for decoding, sight-word vocabulary and comorbid reading disability, with underdeveloped working memory overlapping **41% of the between-group comprehension difference and 85% of the reading-speed difference** ([Kofler et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6301149)) [DOI verified: 10.1007/s10802-018-0447-1]. In university students, a pilot of 16 ADHD versus 30 controls found weaker auditory-verbal working memory ([Gropper & Tannock 2009](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1087054708320390)) [DOI verified: 10.1177/1087054708320390].

Sustained attention is the second pillar, and in adults it may be the proximal one. Adolescents with and without ADHD reading passages in four layouts (standard versus spaced; print versus screen) showed group differences in comprehension and sustained attention, a correlation between the two, and an **interaction between presentation type, spacing and sustained-attention level** ([Stern & Shalev 2013](https://attention.tau.ac.il/publications/published-articles/); summary at [behavioristbookclub](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/assessment-research/the-role-of-sustained-attention-and-display-medium-in-reading-comprehe/)) [DOI verified in a result: 10.1016/j.ridd.2012.08.021; sample size not seen]. A secondary summary adds that double spacing and screen text helped only teens with good sustained attention ([Behaviorist Book Club summary](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/assessment-research/the-role-of-sustained-attention-and-display-medium-in-reading-comprehe/)) [secondary]. In 50 adults with ADHD versus 57 without, short-term memory and vocabulary did not differ and **only sustained attention predicted reading comprehension** ([Segal 2023](https://cris.openu.ac.il/en/publications/sources-of-difficulties-in-reading-comprehension-in-adults-with-a/)) [unverified: author initials, volume and DOI not seen]. In a narrative (not print) task, sustained attention explained more variance in ADHD children's story-structure deficits than inhibition or phonological processing ([Flory et al. 2006](https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/online-story-comprehension-among-children-with-adhd-which-core-de/)) [DOI verified: 10.1007/s10802-006-9070-7].

Mind-wandering is the state-level expression of that attentional lapse. A narrative review argues that deficient default-mode-network deactivation produces excessive spontaneous mind-wandering as a candidate ADHD endophenotype ([Bozhilova et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6525148)) [DOI verified: 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2018.07.010]; in 105 community adults, ADHD symptoms predicted mind-wandering and "detrimental" mind-wandering, with meta-awareness as mediator ([Franklin et al. 2017](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/ref/10.1177/1087054714543494)) [DOI verified: 10.1177/1087054714543494]. The general-population meta-analysis of 25 studies gives a pooled **r = −0.21** between mind-wandering and comprehension, substantially stronger when measured by online thought probes than by trait questionnaires (trait measures yielded a null), and **text length, difficulty, type, interest and working memory were not significant moderators** ([Bonifacci et al. 2023](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9971160/)) [DOI verified: 10.3758/s13423-022-02141-w; author list after first author partly from memory]. The decisive ADHD study is Brann and Sidi's: **210 adults aged 20–50, about half with ADHD**, randomized to metacognitive scaffolding or control while reading a long expository digital text; without scaffolding the ADHD group had lower comprehension and confidence, with scaffolding the groups were comparable, and **state mind-wandering accounted for the comprehension gap in the control condition only** ([Brann & Sidi 2025](https://cris.openu.ac.il/en/publications/metacognitive-scaffolding-for-digital-reading-and-mind-wandering-/); [2023 conference PDF](https://www.openu.ac.il/Lists/MediaServer_Documents/innovation/chais/2023/a1_4.pdf)) [DOI not seen; a 2023 conference version reports n = 315 (43% ADHD), so sample figures conflict between versions]. Zoning out while reading a novella disrupted situation-model construction most when lapses came early ([Smallwood et al. 2008](https://labs.psych.ucsb.edu/schooler/jonathan/node/299)) [DOI not seen]. The 12-item Mind Excessively Wandering Scale offers a mechanism-specific trait measure ([Mowlem et al. 2019](https://gala.gre.ac.uk/id/eprint/32762/)) [DOI not seen; author list partly from memory]; a population validation exists ([Mowlem et al. 2019, Scientific Reports](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10070531/7/Pingault_Evaluating%20a%20scale%20of%20excessive%20mind%20wandering%20among%20males%20and%20females%20with%20and%20without%20attention-deficit%20or%20hyperactivity%20disorder_VoR.pdf)) [DOI not seen]. Trait working memory and state mind-wandering are compatible explanations, since low capacity predicts wandering in general samples; the practical corollary is that a study should measure mind-wandering with thought probes during reading, not with questionnaires.

Which executive function matters most is contested, and so is whether executive function matters at all once language is controlled. In 96 adolescents, planning, flexibility, updating and monitoring each mediated the ADHD comprehension gap separately, but **only monitoring survived joint entry and inhibition did not differ between groups** ([Avramovich & Yeari 2023](https://education.biu.ac.il/en/node/11210); [IUCC record](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/the-role-of-executive-functions-in-reading-comprehension-by-adole-5/)) [DOI conflict between records: 10.1080/10573569.2023.2192705 vs 10.1080/10573569.2022.2103054; page ranges also conflict]. By contrast, a secondary summary of Martinussen and Mackenzie reports that adolescents' comprehension gap was **fully accounted for by vocabulary and word reading**, not teacher-rated executive function ([summary](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/assessment-research/reading-comprehension-in-adolescents-with-adhd--exploring-the-poor-com/)) [unverified: n, DOI not seen; secondary source only], a discrepancy plausibly due to rated rather than performance-based EF. Inattention's link to poor comprehension in 7–11-year-olds was partially mediated by word reading ([Cain & Bignell 2014](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/62139/)) [DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1111/bjep.12009], and a simple-view study of 250 children found decoding and language comprehension explained essentially all comprehension variance in both groups ([PMC10588461](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10588461)) [unverified: authors/year not captured]. Processing speed is the one variable uniquely linked to both reading disability and inattention in multiple-deficit models ([McGrath et al. 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2993430); [Shanahan et al. 2006 record](https://mijn.bsl.nl/processing-speed-deficits-in-attention-disorder-hyperactivity-dis/536786)) [DOIs not seen]. The honest reading of this dispute is that an ADHD reading tool should not assume the reader's problem is attentional until decoding and vocabulary have been checked, which is why the study design below screens for reading disability.

### Adults decode normally but read slowly, re-read heavily and lose the gist

The adult evidence is small-sample but consistent in direction. Twenty-eight adults with ADHD recalled fewer central ideas than 27 controls yet recognised them and rated their importance normally, locating the deficit in **retrieval of central ideas and in forming fewer connections during reading** ([Yeari et al. 2019](https://education.biu.ac.il/en/node/9754); [IUCC record](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/the-origin-of-the-centrality-deficit-in-individuals-with-attentio/)) [DOI not seen]. Eye tracking during uninterrupted text reading shows **significantly longer reading time, far more words re-read more than twice, and first-pass fixations on those words less sensitive to word length**, which the authors propose as a signature of inattentive reading ([Stern et al. 2024](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/unique-patterns-of-eye-movements-characterizing-inattentive-readi/); [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/ref/10.1177/10870547231223728)) [DOI verified: 10.1177/10870547231223728; sample size not seen]. In 110 adults with dyslexia, ADHD or neither, the ADHD group was least impaired on phonology, reading and spelling, and its deficits became non-significant after controlling IQ ([Laasonen et al. 2010](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022219409335216)) [DOI verified: 10.1177/0022219409335216]. Yet on the Nelson-Denny test, 38 college students with ADHD and 38 controls **did not differ in items attempted or correct at standard, 1.5× or 2× time** ([Miller, Lewandowski & Antshel 2015](https://journals.sagepub.com/toc/jada/19/8); [MetaTOC](https://metatoc.com/papers/2191-effects-of-extended-time-for-college-students-with-and-without-adhd)) [DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1177/1087054713483308]. A 34-study scoping review resolves the apparent contradiction: comprehension is impaired in ADHD but the magnitude varies with task demands, with deficits **most apparent on tasks requiring recall of central ideas** ([Parks et al. 2022](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/33851CEA7C1AC6193D088D4D5551ED3C/S1355617725101628a.pdf/reading-without-eye-movements-improving-reading-comprehension-in-young-adults-with-attention-deficithyperactivity-disorder-adhd.pdf)) [DOI verified: 10.1177/10870547211068047]. North American college samples using timed multiple-choice tests on short passages tend toward null results; Israeli lab samples using long expository text with recall and central-idea scoring find consistent deficits. For Clear Read Hub this is the single most important measurement lesson: a comprehension instrument that only asks literal multiple-choice questions on short passages will likely be insensitive to the very deficit the tool targets. Smaller adult studies point the same way, including a Portuguese study of 15 versus 15 undergraduates linking reduced working memory to poorer comprehension ([Diacrítica 2022](https://revistas.uminho.pt/index.php/diacritica/article/view/4779)) [small, regional journal], a 140-undergraduate conference study of BRIEF-A and performance EF ([Markovich 2022](https://library.iated.org/view/MARKOVICH2022EXE)) [conference paper], and a study of 53 adults reporting frequent comprehension and fluency difficulty ([CORE record](https://core.ac.uk/works/9074309)) [authors/year not captured; unverified]. Samuelsson, Lundberg and Herkner (2004) on adult men with ADHD is known only from an author page and an automated summary ([research.com](https://research.com/u/stefan-samuelsson); [Consensus](https://consensus.app/questions/adhd-men/)) [unverified: journal/year/DOI/sample not confirmed by any primary record; treat as unconfirmed]. Medication status is almost never reported in these adult studies, and no study of adults with ADHD reading real-world documents such as forms, contracts or technical documentation was found.

### Comorbid dyslexia in 25–40% of cases forces decoding controls

Dyslexia and ADHD co-occur far above chance: **25–40% of children with one disorder meet criteria for the other**, which the Colorado group reframes under a multiple-deficit model ([Boada, Willcutt & Pennington 2012](https://experts.colorado.edu/display/pubid_53929); [PDF](https://alliedhealth.ceconnection.com/files/UnderstandingtheComorbidityBetweenDyslexiaandAttentionDeficitHyperactivityDisorder-1352212337316.pdf)) [DOI verified: 10.1097/TLD.0b013e31826203ac]. Comorbid children show an additive neuropsychological profile and worse outcomes ([Germanò, Gagliano & Curatolo 2010](https://iris.unime.it/handle/11570/1907750)) [DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1080/87565641.2010.494748]. In a community twin sample of 494 children with reading disability and 373 without, the association was **stronger for inattention than for hyperactivity-impulsivity** ([Willcutt & Pennington 2000](https://scholarhub.vn/publication/Comorbidity-of-Reading-Disability-and-Attention-Deficit-Hyperactivity-Disorder/63fc18e8-f8b3-41da-be2d-7bba2c68575e)) [DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1177/002221940003300206], the comorbidity is substantially genetic ([Willcutt, Pennington & DeFries 2000](https://experts.colorado.edu/display/pubid_52784)) [DOI not seen], and slow processing speed is the shared deficit ([Willcutt et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3079018); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2010/12/101208125805.htm)) [DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1016/j.cortex.2010.06.009]. Grey-matter differences are largely distinct between the disorders ([McGrath & Stoodley 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31752659/)) [DOI not seen]; a four-group eye-tracking study cites comorbidity estimates of 10–45% ([Deans et al. 2010](https://file.scirp.org/Html/2920.html)) [DOI not seen; low-tier open-access publisher], and a South African record of 847 learners exists without a captured rate ([Stark et al. 2022](https://repository.up.ac.za/handle/2263/89687)) [unverified]. No pooled-prevalence meta-analysis and no adult-specific comorbidity figure were found. The methodological consequence is that reviewers will expect ADHD effects to come from designs that control decoding: four-group factorials ([Ghelani et al. 2004](https://scholarhub.vn/publisher/Dyslexia/d5cb32c5-e663-4e98-bfd5-7fc6cd0f778d)) [DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1002/dys.285; n not seen], word-reading-matched controls (Miller et al. 2013), exclusion of reading disorder ([Brown et al. 2011](https://file.scirp.org/Html/7817.html)) [DOI not seen] or "pure" non-medicated samples ([Molina et al. 2020](https://www.ibsgranada.es/en/articulos/children-with-attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-show-an-altered-eye-movement-pattern-during-reading/)) [DOI verified: 10.1097/OPX.0000000000001498], and statistical control of decoding within a dual-task design (Kofler et al. 2019). Because the reading-disability link runs through inattention, screening on inattentive symptoms is the relevant axis.

### Eye movements characterise inattentive reading but cannot diagnose it

Twenty-one non-medicated children with "pure" ADHD showed **more fixations, more regressions, more return-sweep saccades, longer reading time and lower reading rate** than 20 controls on oral reading, a pattern the authors say cannot be attributed to comorbidity ([Molina et al. 2020](https://blogs.ugr.es/claro-group/?p=507)) [DOI verified: 10.1097/OPX.0000000000001498]. Two other child studies disagree: a 96-child Annals of Dyslexia study found the oculomotor pattern of ADHD-without-dyslexia children similar to typical readers ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11881-022-00269-x)) [DOI 10.1007/s11881-022-00269-x located by search], replicated by Donbaloğlu et al. where only the dyslexia group differed ([Wiley](https://ila.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/rrq.70047)) [DOI 10.1002/rrq.70047 located by search]. Discriminant analysis of reading time and saccade direction classified ADHD versus reading disability at **under 60%** accuracy ([Deans et al. 2010](https://file.scirp.org/Html/2920.html); [ISU thesis](https://scholars.indianastate.edu/etds/1307)) [DOI not seen; low-tier open-access publisher], so eye tracking characterises behaviour rather than inferring diagnosis. Adults with ADHD in a non-reading task fixated salient cues normally but scanned more chaotically ([Krejtz et al. 2015, cited in Frontiers 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2024.1482105/pdf)) [secondary citation only]; a preregistered German study of 100 adults reading garden-path sentences has not yet reported results ([Kullik et al., TeaP 2026](https://coms.events/teap26/data/abstracts/en/abstract_0268_0494_0565.html)) [conference abstract; no results]; a Bar-Ilan eye-movement study of planning in adolescents is listed without details ([record](https://education.biu.ac.il/en/node/12060)) [unverified]; and a Turku meta-analysis reports longer, more variable fixations and fewer regressions during mind-wandering episodes ([UTU record](https://www.utupub.fi/items/94d7c802-6f7d-458d-9c32-05d24688a58d)) [authors/year/DOI not captured]. Only two adult ADHD reading eye-tracking studies with results exist (Stern 2024; Yeari 2019), both small, Hebrew-language and single-passage; none covers multi-page or scrolling documents. Stern's re-reading signature nonetheless suggests a usable unobtrusive metric for a product: time spent in multi-pass re-reading as an indicator of attentional lapse.

## Length harms through unfinished reading and mind-wandering, not word count

No peer-reviewed experiment was found that parametrically varied passage length in a diagnosed ADHD sample and measured comprehension, so claims that comprehension declines monotonically with length in ADHD rest on inference. What exists is four converging lines. First, time budgets: in a retrospective chart review of 145 adolescents with ADHD and no reading disorder, **53% could not complete a reading comprehension test under standard time, only 42.8% scored within one SD of their verbal comprehension index, and with extended time 77.9% did (p < 0.001)** ([Brown, Reichel & Quinlan 2011](https://file.scirp.org/Html/7817.html)) [DOI not seen]; the college Nelson-Denny study found no differential benefit (Miller, Lewandowski & Antshel 2015), and typical students gained more from 50% and 100% extra time than students with learning disabilities, so extended time is not disability-specific ([Lewandowski, Cohen & Lovett 2013](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0734282912462693)) [DOI verified: 10.1177/0734282912462693]. Adolescents with ADHD only had adequate single-word reading but subtle difficulties on text reading rate, accuracy and silent comprehension, still within the average range (Ghelani et al. 2004). Second, section length and mind-wandering: 80 undergraduates reading ~500-word passages showed **unintentional mind-wandering of 19% with one-sentence screens versus 24% with 2–6-sentence screens**, with intentional wandering unaffected ([Forrin et al. 2021](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10284384); [Colorado record](https://experts.colorado.edu/display/pubid_285372)) [DOI not seen], although Bonifacci's meta-analysis found no moderation by text length in general samples, which tempers any strong length claim. A thesis found higher ADHD symptoms predicted more inattention during unengaging textbook passages, especially the second one, with no effect on comprehension ([Hurst 2020](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/b6000f50-3ae8-4613-a6eb-19002513f983)) [thesis, not peer-reviewed]. Third, the loss concentrates on central ideas (Miller 2013; Yeari 2019), and think-aloud work shows ADHD adolescents produce fewer deep-processing responses and reinstate fewer prior central ideas ([Yeari & Lavie 2021](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/the-role-of-surface-text-processing-in-centrality-deficit-and-poo/)) [unverified details]; a master's thesis found fewer bridging and elaborative inferences with slower but not less accurate answers ([Avramovich 2015](https://education.biu.ac.il/files/education/shared/avramovichadi.pdf)) [thesis, not peer-reviewed]. Fourth, removing eye movements: undergraduates with ADHD improved comprehension by **almost 13% relative to controls under RSVP**, while controls did worse ([Moussaoui et al. 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/33851CEA7C1AC6193D088D4D5551ED3C/core-reader)) [DOI inferred from Cambridge PII, unverified: 10.1017/S1355617725101628; sample size not seen], a single study that cuts against general-population findings that RSVP harms comprehension ([APS summary](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/were-only-human/read-this-blog-post-in-less-than-a-minute.html)). An early study of 21 ADHD children found lower comprehension but no support for comprehension-monitoring differences ([Brock 1996](https://scholars.csus.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Reading-comprehension-abilities-of-children-with/99257876658401671)) [unverified; co-author "Knapp" not confirmed; journal unconfirmed], and a 2006 chapter reporting no literal/inferential difference but worse recall and ordering could not be attributed to authors ([Emerald](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1016/S0735-004X(06)19009-6)) [could not attribute]. The reference "Hebrew and Hoglund" that the author supplied [could not identify]: no such paper surfaced, and the author is probably recalling the Hebrew-language Tel Aviv (Stern & Shalev) or Bar-Ilan (Yeari) work. The defensible claim for a paper is therefore not "ADHD readers lose comprehension per word" but that ADHD readers take longer, so fixed budgets become incomplete reading; that longer unbroken sections raise unintentional mind-wandering even in typical readers; and that the resulting loss falls on the gist. Chunking, spacing and scaffolded self-monitoring have the clearest mechanistic rationale, but only scaffolding has an ADHD trial behind it.

## Only text-to-speech and scaffolding carry ADHD-specific comprehension evidence

The central fact of the intervention literature is that **ADHD-specific controlled evidence on text presentation is extremely thin**: a handful of studies tested ADHD readers directly, and nearly everything that reading apps market to ADHD users rests on dyslexia or general-population findings. Text-to-speech is the only presentation aid with two independent ADHD-relevant comprehension results. In an eye-tracking study of Taiwanese junior-high students, **TTS significantly improved comprehension for students with ADHD-related reading difficulties but not for those with dyslexia**, who instead gained reduced reading time and mental effort; typically developing students gained nothing and were disrupted, and ADHD students showed longer fixation durations under TTS, read as increased focus ([Chen, Hung & Jian, Reading and Writing](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11145-025-10738-5); [NTNU record](https://scholar.lib.ntnu.edu.tw/en/publications/the-effects-of-text-to-speech-on-reading-comprehension-in-junior-/)) [DOI 10.1007/s11145-025-10738-5 located by search; journal sample not verified, the related thesis reports 60 students]. A Swedish randomized cross-over of **N = 49** children with reading disability found TTS raised reading rate in both grade groups independent of SDQ ADHD score, improved comprehension only in the younger group, and showed a grade-dependent moderation by ADHD symptoms ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0162643417742898)) [DOI 10.1177/0162643417742898 located by search; journal name not confirmed]. Adult evidence is qualitative only: a phenomenological study of AI narration reported better focus and comprehension on complex texts ([JAID](https://edtechbooks.org/jaid_14_2/oawranfbbj)) [not controlled], and a Lund record describes ADHD students' use of audiobooks in higher education ([LUP](https://lup.lub.lu.se/record/67f3db85-dfb1-48af-ba43-a7c32331d253)) [qualitative]. Vendor claims of "25–30% comprehension gains" cite nothing ([text2speech.co](https://text2speech.co/blog/2026/05/text-to-speech-for-adhd/)) [vendor]. The mechanism appears attentional rather than decoding-related, which is exactly why ADHD and dyslexic readers respond differently and why a product should treat TTS as a first-class feature rather than an afterthought.

The medium itself matters for ADHD readers. Forty-five higher-education students with ADHD and 61 matched controls read an expository text on screen or paper; **on screen the ADHD group had lower comprehension and was overconfident, regulated study time poorly only for digital text, and when instructed to highlight improved only in print**; on paper they spent more time but matched controls ([Ben-Yehudah & Brann 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0891422219300605); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332510916_Pay_attention_to_digital_text_The_impact_of_the_media_on_text_comprehension_and_self-monitoring_in_higher-education_students_with_ADHD)) [DOI 10.1016/j.ridd.2019.04.001 located by search; the interventions note did not capture the author list, the HCI note did]. Any digital intervention for ADHD must therefore compensate for weaker self-regulation on screen, which is why scaffolding that externalises monitoring (Brann & Sidi) is the best-evidenced idea in this whole literature. Keyword emphasis has converging small-sample support: bolding the main ideas improved comprehension for 31 dyslexic adults among 62 participants ([Rello, Saggion & Baeza-Yates 2014](https://aclanthology.org/W14-1204)) [DOI 10.3115/v1/W14-1204 located by search], and a multiple-baseline study of **three elementary students with ADHD** found highlighted words reduced inattentive and hyperactive behaviour and increased correct answers ([Santos & Albuquerque 2021](https://periodicos.unb.br/index.php/revistaptp/article/view/22201/34195)) [DOI 10.1590/0102.3772e37302 located by search]. Vendor pages assert that bold-letter reading "significantly improves comprehension" for ADHD without citation ([goblinxadhd](https://www.goblinxadhd.com/blog/unlocking-clarity-mastering-adhd-bold-letter-read/)) [vendor]. The only ADHD-relevant colour evidence is old and secondary: the NCEO toolkit summarises Zentall et al. (2000) as finding moderately better reading-test performance with colour backgrounds for attention-deficit students, and Lee and Zentall (2002) as finding better maths under high stimulation ([NCEO toolkit](https://publications.ici.umn.edu/nceo/accommodations-toolkit/color-contrast-research)) [primary texts not retrieved; secondary]. On pacing, the Moussaoui RSVP result stands against general-reader findings that Spritz impaired literal comprehension and raised visual fatigue ([Benedetto et al. 2015](https://www.tsw.it/en/laboratories-of-experiences/scientific-publications/page/5/)) [DOI not seen; abstract mirror], with a 172-participant reply locating the loss above about **250 wpm** ([Ricciardi & Di Nocera 2017](https://www.datalearner.com/academic/journal-papers/0747-5632/volumes-and-issues/181/paper-detail/36580)) [DOI not seen; listing only]; an arXiv "speed reading tool for ADHD" has no user study ([arXiv 2307.14544](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.14544)) [preprint; unevaluated]. Given the ADHD speed–comprehension trade-off, fixed-pace RSVP is a poor fit unless the user controls pace below that threshold.

### Chunking, headings and spacing are plausible defaults borrowed from other readers

No controlled study of chunking, paragraph length, bullet lists, up-front summaries or progressive disclosure with ADHD readers was found in any search. The only ADHD signal is qualitative: in the 2026 CHI extended abstract, ADHD participants said dense or lengthy text led them to avoid reading, that splitting text into chunks helped them sustain attention, and that they preferred customizable, distraction-free interfaces ([Llana et al. 2026](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3772363.3799383)) [DOI 10.1145/3772363.3799383 on ACM DL URL; one note flags that the DOI and author list came from a single aggregator and were not verified against ACM DL]. For general adults, headings improved memory for subtopics and outlining, while headings and preview sentences did not differ on simple facts ([Lorch, Lemarié & Chen 2013](https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/signaling-topic-structure-via-headings-or-preview-sentences/)) [DOI not seen; record], and topic headings facilitated processing of topic sentences and increased topics mentioned in summaries ([Hyönä & Lorch 2004](https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/1833920?lang=en_US)) [DOI not seen; record]; the SARA framework theorises headings as activating prior knowledge ([Lemarié, Lorch & Péry-Woodley 2012](https://doaj.org/article/b268e54e7dcb49e7b7daa223464636d6)) [record]. No meta-analysis of signalling effects with a pooled effect size was retrieved. App vendors recommend "one paragraph or one line at a time" without data ([Nook](https://getnook.net/blog/how-to-read-with-adhd); [Helperbird](https://www.helperbird.com/blog/best-free-study-tools-for-students-with-adhd/)) [vendor].

Typography evidence for dyslexia is consistent and mostly about spacing. Seventy-four dyslexic children reading with **+2.5 pt letter spacing read more than 20% faster and doubled text accuracy**, with crowding as the proposed mechanism and large individual differences behind the group effect ([Zorzi et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3396504); [reply](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3497756)) [DOI 10.1073/pnas.1205566109 located by search]. In 92 participants (46 dyslexia), larger text and larger character spacing sped both groups ([Rello & Baeza-Yates 2017](https://repositori.upf.edu/items/e0ca6f40-a0fa-490f-9d03-53d043270deb/full)) [DOI 10.1007/s10209-015-0438-8 located by search; an 18 pt "optimal size" appears only in a secondary blog], while an earlier 22-participant study found font size significant, **line spacing not**, and 26 pt best ([Rello et al. 2012](https://a11y-paradise.onrender.com/reviews/69c089c6b56623a3e201b1ee); [W3C note](https://w3.org/WAI/RD/2012/text-customization/r11)) [secondary summary; a methodology-note record gives 28 participants and 18 pt, so figures conflict]. The 48-participant, 12-font eye-tracking study found sans-serif, monospaced and roman styles beat serif, proportional and italic, italics consistently hurt, and **OpenDyslexic was neither better nor worse** with participants preferring Verdana and Helvetica ([Rello & Baeza-Yates 2013](https://a11y-paradise.onrender.com/reviews/69d25a338a41f1818e3d54ca); [BYU summary](https://editingresearch.byu.edu/2020/05/28/which-fonts-are-best-for-dyslexia/)) [DOI 10.1145/2513383.2513447 located by search; summaries only]; the TACCESS follow-up used 97 Spanish speakers and should not be conflated ([Rello & Baeza-Yates 2016](https://a11y-paradise.onrender.com/reviews/69cd7af2bbd7e71582e41407)) [secondary]. Size and spacing benefits also appear in non-dyslexic children ([Masulli et al. 2018](https://hal.parisnanterre.fr/hal-03152723v1)) [record], smaller fonts hurt second but not fifth graders ([Katzir, Hershko & Halamish 2013](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3777945/)) [PMC record], and 12-pt beat 10-pt for general adults ([Bernard et al. 2002 via HFI](https://www.humanfactors.com/Newsletters/more_about_fonts.html)) [secondary summaries only]. Line length work found 95 characters per line fastest and 55 a good compromise ([Dyson & Haselgrove 2001 via Shaikh & Chaparro](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/154193120304701112); [HFI](https://humanfactors.com/newsletters/optimal_line_length.html)) [secondary]. On colour, **Peach, Orange and Yellow backgrounds beat Blue, Blue Grey and Green** for 341 participants (89 dyslexia), with mean dyslexic reading time 14.85 s versus 21.57 s on a secondary page ([Rello & Bigham 2017](https://www.changedyslexia.org/publications/pdfs/2017-ASSETS-Good%20Background%20Colors.pdf)) [figures not verified against PDF]; a 2026 poster adds a cream preference ([OpenU poster](https://www.openu.ac.il/Lists/MediaServer_Documents/innovation/chais/2026/b4_7.pdf)) [poster]. Positive polarity beat dark mode for acuity and proofreading in both younger and older adults, without participants perceiving the difference ([Piepenbrock et al. 2013](https://docserv.uni-duesseldorf.de/servlets/DerivateServlet/Derivate-31413); [Mannheim record](https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/26118/)) [DOI not seen], though the better polarity varies by individual ([arXiv 2409.10841](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.10841)) [preprint]. The one ADHD typography datum is Llana's: ADHD status had a large effect on reading speed, **ADHD participants read faster (p = .029) with about 22% fewer saccades**, speed correlated negatively with pupil dilation (**r = −0.376, p < .001**), and the authors describe a speed–comprehension trade-off; per-font comprehension figures were not retrievable ([Llana et al. 2026](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3772363.3799383); [a11y-paradise](https://a11y-paradise.onrender.com/reviews/69ed2c3f083d4508fd230b16)). A Thai observational study of handwriting-style fonts for ADHD children is low quality ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/369753521_Identification_of_Digital_Font_Size_and_Font_Type_to_Enhance_the_Attention_Span_of_Children_Living_with_ADHD_in_a_Typical_Learning_Environment)) [low quality; method not retrievable]. Because ADHD-without-dyslexia children show typical oculomotor patterns in two studies, crowding-based spacing manipulations may matter less for ADHD than for dyslexia, and engagement manipulations more. Reading rulers isolating a band of lines improved dyslexic readers' speed by about **19, 16 and 12 wpm** for Grey Bar, Shade and Underline among 177 crowd participants with co-occurring ADHD permitted, no ruler suited everyone, and the top reasons were "keep my place" and "focus" ([Niklaus et al. 2023](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3544548.3581367); [Readability Matters](https://readabilitymatters.org/articles/research-highlight-digital-reading-rulers)) [DOI 10.1145/3544548.3581367 located by search; comprehension effect and ADHD subgroup not verified]; a JOV abstract's 86 wpm figure is a different analysis ([JOV](https://jov.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2777804)) [abstract]. Distraction removal is supported indirectly: salient and animated ads capture gaze and disrupt reading ([Simola et al. 2011](https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/3550924?lang=en_US)) [DOI 10.1037/a0024042 located by search], ads nearest the text slow processing ([Frontiers 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00211/full)) [DOI located by search], animated ads reduced nine-year-olds' comprehension **more in children with lower inhibitory control** ([Lund dissertation](https://www.lunduniversity.lu.se/lup/publication/f8c12896-b789-4394-b9cd-1cece113fb08)) [dissertation], while adult comprehension did not differ across distraction conditions ([Lan thesis](https://figshare.mq.edu.au/articles/thesis/Reading_online_text_content_in_the_presence_of_visual_distraction_Eye_movements_and_comprehension/24331030)) [thesis] and animated banners slowed tasks without being more memorable ([Burke et al., TOCHI](https://thoughtcrumbs.com/publications/ToCHI04.pdf)) [PDF]. No study of reader mode or dark mode with ADHD readers exists; since polarity preference varies individually, a toggle rather than a dark default is the evidence-aligned design.

### Simplification helps most groups but has never been tested with ADHD readers

Simplification effects are consistent in direction across populations and roughly **+4 to +11 percentage points** on comprehension questions. Ninety-nine health consumers went from **52% to 63% correct** with term-familiarity simplification ([Leroy et al. 2013](https://www.jmir.org/2013/7/e144); [AMIA/PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3540563)) [JMIR record; DOI not listed in notes]; 243 autistic adolescents and adults scored **11.2 versus 10.0** on Open Book-simplified texts ([Cerga-Pashoja et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6692437)) [DOI 10.3389/fpsyt.2019.00546 located by search]; Google's randomized study of **4,563 readers** across 31 texts found simplified text raised accuracy by about **3.9 points**, largest for PubMed articles (+14.6%), with lower NASA-TLX workload ([arXiv 2505.01980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.01980); [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/llm-based-lossless-text-simplification-and-its-effect-on-user-comprehension-and-cognitive-load/)) [preprint; not peer reviewed at time of search]; and 23 dyslexic readers read faster with more frequent words and understood better with shorter words ([Rello et al. 2013, INTERACT](https://dl.ifip.org/IFIP-LNCS-8120/hal-01510517)) [DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-40498-6_15 located by search]. Dyslexic readers rated text more readable when simpler synonyms were available on demand rather than silently substituted ([DysWebxia 2.0](https://www.changedyslexia.org/publications/pdfs/2013-W4A-DysWebxia%20More%20Accessible%20Text.pdf); [Simplify or help? summary](https://a11y-paradise.onrender.com/reviews/69c080e9fbe1707d6a0f3216)) [DOI 10.1145/2461121.2461126; numerical results not retrieved]. People with intellectual disability show weaker or null effects, and comprehension questions were the most reliable measure among several ([Säuberli et al. 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3613904.3642570); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.13094v1)) [DOI 10.1145/3613904.3642570 located by search]; automatic readability metrics correlate poorly with human judgement and Simple Wikipedia is harder than easy-read corpora ([Štajner / Yaneva et al. 2016](https://aclanthology.org/L16-1045/)) [ACL record; the two notes attribute first authorship differently, verify]. A 2025 intellectual-disability paper ([T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17483107.2025.2536701)) [content not retrieved], the EASIER usability study ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10036960/)) [DOI 10.1007/s10209-023-00986-z located by search], and an audio replication of the simplification effect with 44 listeners ([Barai, Leroy & Ahmed](https://aisel.aisnet.org/cais/vol57/iss1/48)) [in press] round out the picture. A PMC paper on word length and frequency effects in ADHD-I readers exists but its results were not retrieved ([PMC10063363](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10063363/)) [results not retrieved]. **No simplification or summarization user study with ADHD participants was found**, and no study of up-front summaries for cognitive accessibility either. The reduced-working-memory-load mechanism makes simplification a plausible ADHD aid, and the dyslexia preference for on-demand rather than silent substitution matches ADHD users' stated wish for control.

### Personalisation beats presets, and self-report is a poor guide to the optimum

The Adobe/Brown readability programme found that among 386 crowdsourced adults, **reading speed differed by 35% between a person's fastest and slowest font with no comprehension change**, individual differences of about 75 wpm, and materials released ([Wallace et al. 2022](https://research.adobe.com/publication/towards-individuated-reading-experiences-different-fonts-increase-reading-speed-for-different-individuals); [TOCHI PDF](https://jeffhuang.com/papers/Readability_TOCHI22.pdf)) [DOI 10.1145/3502222 per recollection, NOT VERIFIED IN THIS SESSION]; in the CHI 2020 precursor, 77% of 63 participants believed their preferred font would be fastest but this held for only about **20%** ([Readability Matters](https://readabilitymatters.org/articles/individual-differences-in-font-preference-effectiveness); [Adobe blog](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2022/05/10/the-need-to-personalize-fonts-for-each-individual-reader)) [secondary], and FontMART recommendations sped readers by 14–25 wpm ([Adobe](https://www.adobe.com/se/corporate-responsibility/readability.html)) [vendor page; venue/authors not confirmed]. Rello's IDEAL reader let 47 users adjust size, spacing, colours and on-demand synonyms with higher readability ratings ([ASSETS 2013 PDF](https://www.changedyslexia.org/publications/pdfs/2013-ASSETS-An%20iOS%20Reader%20Dyslexia.pdf)) [PDF]. Together with the rulers result and the polarity variation, this argues for measured within-user A/B recommendation rather than a static "ADHD profile", and because ADHD readers trade speed for comprehension, any adaptive objective must be comprehension-at-speed rather than speed alone. No adaptive or personalised reading-interface study with ADHD participants and measured outcomes was found.

### Bionic Reading, dyslexia fonts, overlays and Sans Forgetica fail controlled tests

Every controlled test of fixation bolding is null. Readwise timed **2,074 readers at 325.3 wpm with Bionic versus 327.9 wpm plain**, under 1% and with identical comprehension ([Readwise 2022](https://blog.readwise.io/bionic-reading-results/); [Quartz](https://qz.com/2182666/does-bionic-reading-work-typographers-and-scientists-weigh-in)) [industry blog, not peer reviewed]; 32 students read 100 paragraphs with no reading-time difference ([Snell 2024](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/no-bionic-reading-does-not-work/)) [DOI 10.1016/j.actpsy.2024.104304 located by search]; a registered report with **90 skilled readers** found bolding pulled first fixations toward word starts without changing speed, skipping or comprehension ([Parker et al. 2026](https://dx.doi.org/10.1098/rsos.250177); [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-bionic-faster-scientists.html)) [DOI 10.1098/rsos.250177 located by search]; Bionic formatting lengthened reading time on screen and gave no paper advantage ([Možina et al. 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/21582440251376158)) [DOI 10.1177/21582440251376158; sample size not retrieved]; and a 2025 PMC paper found no eye-movement change ([PMC12565662](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12565662/)) [journal not confirmed]. Secondary mentions of Spear et al. 2025 and Beelders 2025 could not be confirmed ([ScienceBlog](https://scienceblog.com/?p=600457)) [secondary], no "Hayes" preprint on Bionic Reading was found, and a Utrecht EEG thesis with ADHD adults was inconclusive ([Utrecht](https://studenttheses.uu.nl/handle/20.500.12932/47156)) [thesis]. No Bionic study has tested ADHD or dyslexic readers; the popular claim rests on nothing. Specialist fonts fare no better: OpenDyslexic produced no rate or accuracy gain in an alternating-treatment design and no child preferred it ([Wery & Diliberto 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5629233/)) [DOI 10.1007/s11881-016-0127-1 located by search; the commonly cited n = 12 could not be verified]; Dyslexie "neither benefits nor impedes" ([Kuster et al. 2018](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Dyslexie-font-does-not-benefit-reading-in-children-Kuster-Weerdenburg/ff4528589d7a038b4c3f0f297d8ae0826d053538)) [record]; a ~7% Dyslexie advantage in 39 low-progress readers **disappeared when Arial's spacing was matched** ([Marinus et al. 2016](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/a-special-font-for-people-with-dyslexia-does-it-work-and-if-so-wh/)) [record]; and 71 children (37 dyslexia) showed no word or passage difference with spacing held constant ([Joseph & Powell 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/dys.1727)) [DOI 10.1002/dys.1727 located by search]. Reading Rockets and the IDA concur ([Shanahan](https://www.readingrockets.org/blogs/shanahan-on-literacy/what-about-special-fonts-kids-dyslexia-or-other-reading-problems); [IDA](https://dyslexiaida.org/do-special-fonts-help-people-with-dyslexia/)) [secondary]; the Dyslexie vendor's research page is not independent ([dyslexiefont.com](https://dyslexiefont.com/research)) [vendor]. Coloured overlays sped jumbled-text reading equally in both groups, with no connected-text gain and shrinking effects at retest ([Henderson, Tsogka & Snowling 2013](https://www.psy.ox.ac.uk/publications/385403); [White Rose](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/85990/)) [DOI not seen], against older Wilkins claims of ~25% ([Frontiers 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00833/pdf)) [secondary] and a registered trial with no results found ([NCT02680223](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02680223)). Sans Forgetica produced no memory benefit and sometimes impaired recall ([Taylor et al. 2020](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/136656); [BPS](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/hard-read-font-was-designed-boost-memory-it-might-not-actually-work)) [DOI not seen]. Popular claims that lack any controlled evidence include "ADHD fonts" such as Lexend and Atkinson Hyperlegible ([getnook](https://getnook.net/blog/best-fonts-for-adhd-reading); [designyourway](https://www.designyourway.net/blog/best-fonts-for-adhd/); [alexandria.live](https://alexandria.live/use-cases/reading-fonts)) [vendor/blog; one page itself states no peer-reviewed ADHD font study exists], BeeLine for ADHD ([Behavioral Scientist](https://behavioralscientist.org/speed-reading-apps-open-research-questions/)) [secondary], dark mode for ADHD focus, and chunking or reader mode for ADHD. No evidence for Lexend was found in either direction, and Dobres et al. (2017) on polarity could not be verified.

A defensible evidence-based ADHD reading product would offer TTS and self-monitoring scaffolds as first-class features, emphasise key terms, keep plain sans-serif fonts with generous size and spacing as low-risk defaults, strip animated peripheral content, default to light polarity with a toggle, let users personalise with measurement rather than presets, avoid Bionic-style bolding and fixed-pace RSVP, and label chunking, reader mode and simplification honestly as plausible but untested for ADHD.

## HCI has studied ADHD focus and tasks, but almost never document reading

The HCI and accessibility literature on ADHD has grown sharply since about 2022, but only two 2026 papers directly test reading interfaces with ADHD participants, and both are small. Llana and colleagues' CHI EA '26 study ran **21 participants, 6 with ADHD**, screened with the ASRS-v1.1, each reading four eighth-grade passages in randomly assigned Roboto variants and sizes with five-question quizzes, and released an open-source stimulus generator, ReadGen; its design recommendations are chunked text, clear sectioning, and avoidance of intrusive scrolling and clutter ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3772363.3799383); [a11y-paradise](https://a11y-paradise.onrender.com/reviews/69ed2c3f083d4508fd230b16)) [peer-reviewed, extended abstract; DOI/author list from one aggregator flagged]; a preliminary 3-participant poster found no font-size–pupil relationship ([VSS 2025](https://visionsciences.org/presentation/?id=2470)) [weak evidence]. "Too Long to Read?" built Adapt AI, which restructures LLM responses via condensation, visual hierarchy and an original/adapted toggle at the presentation layer; **all 10 participants with attention-related challenges said the adapted view was easier to read and 7 of 10 called it more accessible**, with the authors noting no comparison group and demand characteristics, and concluding that benefit comes from reorganising for scanning and agency, not shortening ([Lee et al., C&C '26](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3803784.3816862)) [peer-reviewed, short paper; DOI 10.1145/3803784.3816862]. The closest analogue is video: FocusView let 12 ADHD viewers customise layout, captions and audio, with perceived viewability improving (**F = 165.4, p < 0.001, η²p = 0.75**), ease of use 6.67 and customisation effort 1.54, and the design tension that too many controls make customisation itself a distraction ([Zhu, Chen & Zhao, ASSETS '25](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3663547.3746386); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13309)) [peer-reviewed; a third-party review alleges a confounded half/half design, unverified]; a 16-participant think-aloud found overly dense lecture visuals hinder ADHD learners who then use AI tools to restructure content ([arXiv 2609.09443](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09443)) [preprint; author list not retrieved].

The rest of ADHD HCI concerns studying and task management. A mixed-methods ASSETS '25 study of STEM doctoral students with ADHD (5 interviews plus 9 experience-sampling participants; ACM lists 13) frames hyperfocus as a strength institutions fail to support ([Ezeamii & Shinohara 2025](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3663547.3746325)) [peer-reviewed]; a Reddit analysis identifies body doubling and collaborative emotional support as access practices ([Piper et al., ASSETS '25](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3663547.3746324)) [peer-reviewed]; 22 interviews plus a 20-person speed-dating study find task management relational and emotional and call for socially aware AI ([Chen, Meng & Nie, CSCW 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17258); [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3816978)) [peer-reviewed; arXiv version]; a survey of 49 adults with ADHD on knowledge work found persistent prioritisation and time-estimation difficulty and appetite for AI tools ([Campbell, Çerçi & Cecchinato, INTERACT 2023](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-61688-4_10)) [peer-reviewed]; and among 137 students, inattentive symptoms predicted better quiz scores for device note-takers and worse for handwriters ([Shimko, Fyfe & James 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/acp.70105?af=R)) [peer-reviewed]. The framing literature matters for review: Spiel and colleagues' insider critical review found ADHD technologies largely "mitigate" experiences seen as disruptive to neurotypical norms and rarely co-design with ADHD people ([Spiel et al., CHI '22](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3491102.3517592); [UvA](https://dare.uva.nl/id/b5158a6b-ee52-48a7-b4be-772d430bad4a)) [peer-reviewed]; a PRISMA-ScR scoping review screened **3,538 records to 46 papers** and found most adopt a therapeutic rather than support lens ([Tan et al., AT@WORK 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21791v1)) [preprint/workshop]; a Frontiers scoping review covers 133 digital-health studies for adult ADHD ([Frontiers 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2026.1746732/full)) [peer-reviewed]; an earlier systematic review found most ADHD HCI studies report age and gender but never race, and only three had control groups ([arXiv 2005.09993](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.09993)) [preprint]. Critical-disability work on ADHD communities ([Eagle & Ringland, ASSETS '23](https://www.sigaccess.org/newsletter/2023-01/frontmatter.html)) [peer-reviewed], body doubling ([arXiv 2605.07851](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07851); [arXiv 2509.12153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12153)) [preprints; ASSETS '23 poster DOI not retrieved], intersectional lived experience ([Le, ASSETS '24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04500)) [peer-reviewed + preprint], children's care ecosystems ([CHI '23](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3544548.3581216)) [peer-reviewed], and fidgeting robots ([ASSETS '24](https://assets24.sigaccess.org/program/schedule/)) [programme listing] complete the field. On LLM use, an analysis of 61 neurodivergent Reddit communities found many ADHD users distil long texts with LLMs but complain of overly neurotypical responses ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3701194); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06336v1)) [peer-reviewed; DOI 10.1145/3701194; author list not retrieved], and 15 neurodivergent job-seekers found chatbots imposed neurotypical language ([ASSETS '25](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3663547.3746361)) [peer-reviewed]. The searches did not surface any ADHD-reading paper by Kaziunas, Pfister, Zhang, Motahar or Kolesnichenko, so their involvement is unverified, and an ASSETS 2026 paper on adult students with ADHD was mentioned without title. The convergent design pattern across the three direct studies is "structure, don't just shorten": chunking, visual hierarchy, a small set of user toggles, and an always-available original view, offered as design hypotheses rather than established effects. Reviewers at CHI and ASSETS will expect non-deficit framing and some participatory input.

### Readability models exist for dyslexia and intellectual disability, none for ADHD

Population-specific readability modelling is best established for dyslexia through Rello's DysWebxia line, built on more than **20 eye-tracking experiments with 240 participants (120 with dyslexia)** and integrated into Text4All and the IDEAL reader ([UPF thesis](https://repositori.upf.edu/items/960d188c-0745-45fa-9e84-55f9c6973879); [W4A 2013](https://www.changedyslexia.org/publications/pdfs/2013-W4A-DysWebxia%20More%20Accessible%20Text.pdf)) [thesis/record], including a W4A 2015 model predicting dyslexia from eye-tracking ([Rello & Ballesteros](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2745555.2746644)) [peer-reviewed; DOI 10.1145/2745555.2746644], a position paper claiming text with 16% word errors gave dyslexic readers higher comprehension than controls ([a11y-paradise](https://a11y-paradise.onrender.com/reviews/69bf2c89ad2e732db515c686)) [secondary], and a neural generation framework whose details were not retrieved ([ACM 2024/25](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3696593.3696625)) [peer-reviewed; details not retrieved]. For intellectual disability, Yaneva's EasyRead and LocalNews corpora ([LREC 2016](https://aclanthology.org/L16-1045/); [RANLP 2015](https://preview.aclanthology.org/moar-dois/R15-2005.pdf)) [peer-reviewed] and the EASIER glossary interface ([Moreno et al. 2023](https://e-archivo.uc3m.es/entities/publication/c6642351-625c-4894-bf0c-f7888bfe0c86)) [peer-reviewed; DOI 10.1007/s10209-023-00986-z] lead. Personalised modelling shows reader-level models beat population-level ones even for word complexity ([Gooding & Tragut 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.02564v1)) [preprint; Findings of NAACL 2022 venue NOT VERIFIED IN THIS SESSION], echoed by a Louvain thesis ([DIAL](https://dial-mem.test.bib.ucl.ac.be/handle/2078.2/27976)) [thesis], Bergsma and Yarowsky's native-language adaptation ([ATALA](https://www.atala.org/node/691)) [listing], a health-resource IEEE Access model ([UTS](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/153185)) [peer-reviewed], and newer preprints on code readability ([arXiv 2503.07870](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.07870)), eye-tracking evaluation of readability assessment ([arXiv 2502.11150](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.11150)) and GPT-4 readability modification on CLEAR ([arXiv 2410.14028](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.14028)) [preprints]. LLM adaptation evaluated with disabled users remains tiny and uncontrolled: MTCAI with **5 adults with IDD** ([Benson-Goldberg et al. 2026](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jppi.70061)) [peer-reviewed; DOI 10.1111/jppi.70061], Adapt AI with 10, SimplifyMyText with evaluation details not retrieved ([Springer](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1007/978-3-031-88717-8_32)) [peer-reviewed], Llama 2 on Spanish Easy-to-Read with expert evaluation only ([Martínez, Ramos & Moreno 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2024.1394705/pdf)) [peer-reviewed], CLEARS-2025 shared-task systems with automatic metrics ([arXiv 2508.03240](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.03240v1); [arXiv 2509.17209](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.17209)) [preprints], a human-in-the-loop scientific-summary corpus ([arXiv 2607.25630](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25630)), a framework measuring how frontier LLMs adapt to neurodivergence prompts without a user study ([arXiv 2605.00113](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.00113)), syntactically driven dynamic font sizing ([arXiv 2504.09734](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09734)), THERIF readability themes ([arXiv 2303.04221](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.04221)) and ADHD detection from transcripts ([arXiv 2505.21324](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21324v1)) [all preprints]. **No LLM document-adaptation paper evaluated with a diagnosed-ADHD sample under controlled conditions was found**, and no "ADHD readability model" grounded in empirical data exists.

### W3C, ISO and EN 301 549 offer expert guidance, not tested ADHD requirements

WCAG 2.2 addresses text readability mainly through SC 1.4.12 Text Spacing at Level AA, which requires no loss of content when users set **line height ≥ 1.5×, paragraph spacing ≥ 2×, letter spacing ≥ 0.12× and word spacing ≥ 0.16× font size** ([W3C Understanding 1.4.12](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/text-spacing.html); [thewcag.com](https://www.thewcag.com/criteria/1.4.12)) [standard; secondary]. The criteria most relevant to ADHD readers are AAA and so rarely legally required: 1.4.8 Visual Presentation, whose Understanding text notes that people with cognitive disabilities lose their place in hard-to-read layouts and benefit from reflow ([W3C 1.4.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/visual-presentation.html); [WCAG 2.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/UNDERSTANDING-WCAG20/visual-audio-contrast-visual-presentation.html); [WebAIM](https://webaim.org/standards/wcag/WCAG2Checklist.pdf)) [standard; line-spacing figure disputed between secondary sources], and 3.1.5 Reading Level, which asks for a supplement when text exceeds lower-secondary level ([pageperson.org](https://pageperson.org/resources/cognitive-accessibility-standards-wcag/); [accessibility.build](https://accessibility.build/guides/cognitive-accessibility)) [secondary]. The COGA Note "Making Content Usable" (2021) organises eight objectives, notably "use clear and understandable content", "help users maintain focus" and "support adaptation and personalization" ([W3C TR](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable); [Design Guide](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/design_guide.html)) [standard], explicitly lists AD(H)D among groups to include in usability testing ([Usability Testing](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/usability_testing.html)) [standard], and carries ADHD personas: Carolyn, a yoga teacher pulling key points from dense pages ([personas](https://w3c.github.io/coga/persona/); [Appendix A 2020](https://www.w3.org/TR/2020/WD-coga-usable-20201211/appendix_a.html)), "Yuki" in the current TR with mirrored text ([use cases](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/use_cases.html)) [naming inconsistency; verify], Paula in the user-research document ([coga-user-research](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-user-research/)) and Stefan in WAI's user stories ([WAI](https://www.w3.org/WAI/people-use-web/user-stories/)) [standard]. Its evidence base, the "Cognitive Accessibility Research Modules", is a First Public Working Draft dated **5 February 2026** that lists ADHD and "intends to add more research" ([W3C TR](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-research-modules/); [announcement](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-review-announce/2026Feb/0002.html)) [standard, early draft]. COGA patterns are expert consensus, not validated interventions, and a tool that adapts spacing, structure and reading level is implementing guidance most mainstream content does not meet.

Other standards operate at a similar altitude. ISO 24495-1:2023 gives plain-language principles for text and defers digital accessibility to WCAG and EN 301 549 ([ISO](https://committee.iso.org/standard/78907.html?browse=ics); [Accessible EU Centre](https://accessible-eu-centre.ec.europa.eu/content-corner/digital-library/iso-24495-12023-plain-language-part-1-governing-principles-and-guidelines_en)) [standard]; ISO 21801-1:2020 on cognitive accessibility was confirmed current in 2025 ([ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/71711.html?browse=tc)) [standard]; EN 301 549 handles cognition only through functional performance statement 4.2.10 because its drafters could not find testable, widely accepted cognitive requirements ([ETSI PDF](https://www.etsi.org/deliver/etsi_en/301500_301599/301549/02.01.01_20/en_301549v020101a.pdf); [Pluke 2015](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-cognitive-a11y-tf/2015Feb/0080.html); [Accessible Canada](https://accessible.canada.ca/node/379)) [standard]; Inclusion Europe's Easy-to-Read rules underpin EU easy-read content in 16 languages ([Inclusion Europe](https://www.inclusion-europe.eu/easy-to-read-standards-guidelines/); [EU Publications Office](https://op.europa.eu/en/web/webguide/plain-language-and-easy-to-read)) [standard]; the UK Home Office posters have no ADHD poster, though the 2018 set was tested with ADHD staff ([GDS 2016](https://accessibility.blog.gov.uk/2016/09/02); [Home Office 2018](https://hodigital.blog.gov.uk/2018/05/17/illustrating-the-importance-of-accessible-design/)) [government]; and Microsoft's Inclusive Design toolkit has a Cognition Guide and Mental Health extension ([Microsoft](https://inclusive.microsoft.design/articles/inclusive-design-for-cognition-guidebook); [Microsoft Design](https://microsoft.design/articles/inclusive-designs-next-wave-breaks-for-mental-health/)) [vendor]. None contains ADHD-specific testable requirements for reading interfaces; a tool can claim alignment but not conformance except via WCAG mapping.

### Datasets carry teacher and editor judgements, never ADHD readers'

OneStopEnglish offers **189 texts at three levels under CC BY-SA 4.0**, the only licence positively confirmed ([Vajjala & Lučić 2018](https://preview.aclanthology.org/fix_video/W18-0535.pdf)) [peer-reviewed]. Newsela requires academic affiliation, publication intent and an NDA ([Newsela](https://newsela.com/legal/data)) [vendor policy; Xu et al. TACL 2015 NOT VERIFIED IN THIS SESSION]; WeeBit's licence and location were not found ([described in arXiv 1907.11779](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.11779)) [Vajjala & Meurers 2012 NOT VERIFIED IN THIS SESSION]. Dyslexia eye-tracking datasets exist for 70 Czech children ([ETDD70, SISAP 2025](https://ped.muni.cz/en/research/research-and-development/publications/2429757)) [peer-reviewed; DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-75823-2_3; licence not found] and Danish readers ([CopCo](https://aclanthology.org/people/marina-bjornsdottir/unverified/)) [licence not found], alongside Gooding and Tragut's word-complexity benchmark and the Kaggle CommonLit competition data ([HF mirror](https://huggingface.co/datasets/JasperYOU/DatawiseAgent-benchmarkdata/blob/main/DataModeling/task/commonlitreadabilityprize.txt)). **No corpus contains readability judgements from ADHD readers.** For Clear Read Hub, OneStopEnglish is the safe open stimulus source, CLEAR is usable after a licence check, and an ADHD-rated readability dataset would itself be a publishable contribution.

### Commercial ADHD reading tools have no independent ADHD evidence

No tool has independent peer-reviewed evidence of benefit for ADHD readers. BeeLine Reader claims "remarkable results" and a 69%-more-reading Perlego trial without a source ([beelinereader.com](https://beelinereader.com/)) [vendor]; a WestEd study of **530 seventh-graders** found engagement and comprehension gains but was not ADHD-focused ([WestEd 2018](https://www.rif.org/sites/default/files/documents/2019/10/24/Support_Materials/BeeLineWestEdStudyFinal.pdf)) [report, not peer reviewed]; the one peer-reviewed study found second graders read slower and third graders' comprehension may suffer ([CHB Reports 2022](https://doaj.org/article/b48617b6416f4030ae9bf57ab79d1318)) [peer-reviewed]; a Leiden study's headline was unretrievable ([Leiden](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/handle/1887/3448262)). Microsoft Immersive Reader was designed in consultation with ADHD students but its ESSA Level 4 report cites generic dyslexia spacing research ([Microsoft ESSA](https://microsoft.com/en-us/education/msdownloads/Microsoft_Immersive%20Reader_ESSA_Level_4_Report_2023.pdf)) [vendor], with small dyslexia trials ([BDA coverage](https://bda.ten4dev.com/news/trial-finds-using-microsoft-s-one-note-software-boosts-dyslexic-children-s-learning); [JELT 2023](https://journals.eltai.in/jelt/article/download/JELT650502/990/1006); [Zero Project](https://zeroproject.org/view/project/1b54d4b6-8e17-eb11-a813-000d3ab9b226)) [secondary/small]. Readwise's Bionic test is above; an Iranian EFL study reporting gains ([Khazaee et al. 2025](https://journals.razi.ac.ir/article_3914.html)) and a Utrecht thesis ([Utrecht](https://studenttheses.uu.nl/handle/20.500.12932/47512)) are weak. Helperbird calls its tools "evidence-based" without citing a study and offers anecdotes about faster voices ([Helperbird](https://www.helperbird.com/help/how-to-use-helperbird-for-adhd-and-focus/); [ADCET](https://www.adcet.edu.au/resource/10826/helperbird-accessibility-dyslexia-software)) [vendor]; Speechify's ADHD pages are CEO-authored marketing ([Speechify](https://speechify.com/vi/blog/adhd-reading-tools/)) [vendor]; a four-student TTS fluency study is too small and mixed ([LDMJ](https://js.sagamorepub.com/index.php/ldmj/article/view/5278)) [small]. The largest evaluation of any population-specific reading tool remains Open Book's 243-participant cross-over for autism. The paper should not cite these products as efficacy evidence and must plan its own comparison condition, since even the academic ADHD interface studies lack controls.

## A preregistered within-subject study of 24–36 screened adults would exceed field norms

Published reading-interface comparisons with neurodivergent readers are overwhelmingly within-subject: each participant reads several comparable passages, one per presentation condition, with reading speed, eye-tracking metrics and multiple-choice comprehension as primary outcomes plus preference. Rello's 48-participant, 12-font study and the 92-participant size/spacing study fit this template ([Rello & Baeza-Yates 2013](https://a11y-paradise.onrender.com/reviews/69d25a338a41f1818e3d54ca); [Rello & Baeza-Yates 2017](https://repositori.upf.edu/items/e0ca6f40-a0fa-490f-9d03-53d043270deb)) [counterbalancing not confirmable from snippets; check full papers], as do Wallace's crowdsourced font study and the CHI EA '26 ADHD study, which assigned four passages to conditions randomly. The EMTeC corpus illustrates the cleanest item assignment, a Latin square of 42 items across 14 lists so each participant sees each item once and each condition equally often, with order randomised within list ([arXiv 2408.04289](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.04289)) [preprint]. Three cautions come from adjacent work: a critique of Rello's corpus notes that only half of participants could identify which settings made them faster, that readability and comprehension needed separate treatment, and that dyslexic participants' data "never showed normal distributions" ([a11y-paradise review](https://a11y-paradise.onrender.com/reviews/69bf2c89ad2e732db515c686)) [secondary], so the analysis plan should prespecify linear mixed-effects models with participant and passage as random effects and report effect sizes with intervals; IBM's 82-participant study found no serif/sans-serif difference ([Beymer, Russell & Orton](https://www.scienceopen.com/document_file/671b2d53-73ce-4a91-946d-fde0b7db381a/ScienceOpen/015_Beymer.pdf); [IBM](https://research.ibm.com/publications/an-eye-tracking-study-of-how-font-size-and-type-influence-online-reading)) [record] and a 32-participant typography study found font irrelevant but full justification costly ([Jarosch, Schlesewsky & Füssel 2017](https://ids-pub.bsz-bw.de/files/10274/Jarosch_Schlesewsky_Fuessel_Taking_typography_to_experimental_testing_2017.pdf)) [PDF], which argues against spending conditions on fonts; and because ADHD participants read faster with fewer saccades in the CHI EA study, **speed alone is an ambiguous outcome for ADHD** and must be paired with comprehension and probe-caught mind-wandering. Thought probes are the established low-cost add-on: Brann and Sidi's 210-adult study, Hurst's thesis, and a PLoS ONE study in which high-inattention college students reported more mind-wandering during reading, linked to poorer comprehension ([Jonkman et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/plo/pone00/0181213.html)) [record; authors/year partial]. A Devanagari font study with 14 participants ([Ralekar et al.](https://research.iitj.ac.in/publication/effect-of-devanagari-font-type-in-reading-comprehension)) [record] shows the small-n norm the study should beat.

Translating the evidence into a concrete design for Clear Read Hub: recruit adults with ADHD and a matched comparison group; use long expository passages (OneStopEnglish or licence-checked CLEAR, around eighth-grade level as in the CHI EA study) rather than short test items, since the ADHD deficit emerges with long text and gist; test a small number of conditions with mechanistic rationale, such as a plain baseline, chunked text with headings and visual hierarchy, keyword emphasis, optional TTS, and an LLM-restructured view with an original toggle, while leaving out Bionic bolding, specialist fonts and fixed-pace RSVP, which the evidence already answers; score comprehension on central versus peripheral idea units in the Miller and Yeari tradition alongside multiple-choice items; insert thought probes during reading; record time on task and, if eye tracking is available, Stern's multi-pass re-reading rate; and add NASA-TLX, whose six subscales have been used with ADHD participants in a dark-patterns study ([arXiv 2503.05263](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.05263)) [preprint] and with 100 dyslexic readers ([JRP](https://jrp.lcwu.edu.pk/index.php/JRP/article/view/91)) [record], SUS with its 70–100 acceptability band ([SciTePress 2021](https://scitepress.org/PublishedPapers/2021/104528); [SciTePress 2024](https://scitepress.org/publishedPapers/2024/123040/pdf/index.html)) [records], and a preference ranking interpreted cautiously because preference predicts the fastest setting only about a fifth of the time. No ADHD reading-interface study reporting NASA-TLX or SUS was found, so these would be firsts. Preference and satisfaction responses in accessibility research skew positive because participants downplay difficulties and any improvement feels like progress; ASSETS '21 interviews with 27 researchers give guidelines by research question ([Ming et al. 2021](https://a11y-paradise.onrender.com/reviews/69b7f1ba1dd6cd9733e25dd2)) [DOI 10.1145/3441852.3471216; secondary summary], and McDonnell's ASSETS '23 guide on centring disabled people ([a11y-paradise](https://a11y-paradise.onrender.com/reviews/69b0aab768a1bab28c8d9f0a)) [secondary] plus Spiel's critique imply consulting ADHD readers on stimuli and interface before the trial.

### Screen with ASRS, report diagnosis source and medication, and screen out dyslexia

The ASRS v1.1 six-item screener was selected by stepwise logistic regression from 18 DSM-IV items and validated against blind clinical ratings in 154 NCS-R respondents, where the unweighted screener outperformed the full scale on **sensitivity (68.7% vs 56.3%), specificity (99.5% vs 98.3%) and kappa (0.76 vs 0.58)** ([Kessler et al. 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15841682/); [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/psychological-medicine/article/abs/world-health-organization-adult-adhd-selfreport-scale-asrs-a-short-screening-scale-for-use-in-the-general-population/28DF9AC948CE49D49B42AE9DABA325C1)) [DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1017/S0033291704002892; author list partly from memory]; the ASRS v1.1 pilot reported α = 0.88–0.89 ([Adler 2006, Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/694650102/adler2006)) [low-quality host; verify]. The DSM-5 successor uses six items including two executive-dysfunction items and reports **sensitivity 91.4% and specificity 96.0% in the general population but 91.9% and 74.0% in a clinical sample**, with a positive screen at 14 or higher per a secondary source ([Ustun et al. 2017](https://hcp.hms.harvard.edu/publication/world-health-organization-adult-attention-deficithyperactivity-disorder-self-report); [Duke](https://scholars.duke.edu/individual/pub1290227); [adhdinadults.com](https://adhdinadults.com/adhd-screener/)) [DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2017.0298; cutoff and unweighted-scoring recommendation from secondary sources]. Validations elsewhere give AUC 0.916 with 85.2%/89.7% in Turkey ([Koç](https://research.hub.ku.edu.tr/entities/publication/df39585e-1b75-4e53-8366-50946edfb819/full); [PMC8909251](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8909251)), 95.6%/72.3% in German primary care ([LMU](https://oa-fund.ub.uni-muenchen.de/id/eprint/389)), and 79.3%/70.3% in an alcohol-dependent sample ([JAD](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/ref/10.1177/1087054716646450)) [records]: specificity drops sharply in high-prevalence or comorbid samples, which a self-selected "ADHD reading study" sample will be. CAARS (66-item long form; German α .74–.95, test-retest .85–.92) measures current severity ([Cambridge](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/european-psychiatry/article/abs/german-validation-of-the-conners-adult-adhd-rating-scales-caars-ii-reliability-validity-diagnostic-sensitivity-and-specificity/156D836F77944AFB142AC934828FD8EC); [UB](https://diposit.ub.edu/items/35f54aab-712a-4e2e-9525-26bff8530fa3/full)) [original 1999 manual citation from memory], the WURS measures childhood history and its subscales mostly do not correlate with CAARS ([PubMed 33493947](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33493947/)) [record], a medRxiv review calls the ASRS "the most promising" research option ([medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.05.30.23290708.full.pdf)) [preprint], and the DIVA-5 interview achieved 92% accuracy against psychiatrist diagnosis in Korea ([PMC7567566](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7567566)) [DOI not seen] with a Persian study finding 38% versus 55% on SCID-5 ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/1087054720930816)) [DOI verified: 10.1177/1087054720930816]; no English-language DIVA-5 validation was found. Self-report diagnosis carries documented false-positive risk ([PMC12188335](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12188335/)) [record], although MTurk's self-reported ADHD prevalence matched offline samples across 6,526 workers ([Peeref](https://peeref.com/works/735188)) [record] and **13.9%** of US working-age adults now self-report an ADHD diagnosis ([Syracuse](https://www.maxwell.syr.edu/research/article/self-reported-adhd-diagnosis-status-among-working-age-adults-in-the-united-states)) [secondary summary]. The reporting standard that follows is to state diagnosis source, age at diagnosis and medication status on the test day, an ASRS score for every participant including controls, optionally CAARS-S and WURS-25, the MEWS as a mechanism measure, comorbidities including dyslexia with a brief decoding check given the 25–40% overlap, and accommodations offered; a self-report-only group should be described as "adults with self-reported ADHD diagnosis who screened positive on ASRS". Language must follow the SIGACCESS Accessible Writing Guide ([SIGACCESS](https://www.sigaccess.org/welcome-to-sigaccess/resources/accessible-writing-guide/)) and the ASSETS CFP, which tells authors to avoid "vulnerable", "special needs", "suffering from", "normal" and "X challenged" ([assets23 CFP](https://assets23.sigaccess.org/call-for-papers.html)) [venue page], noting that existing guides may be out of date ([Levy et al. 2021](https://wildlab.cs.washington.edu/Publications_files/Levy_ASSETS2021.pdf)) [PDF], with recruitment through disability organisations ([Wattenberg 2005](https://www.sigaccess.org/?p=284); [May et al. 2025](https://researchwith.njit.edu/en/publications/participant-recruitment-in-accessibility-research/)) [records]. No official SIGACCESS or SIGCHI document titled "Guidelines for research with people with disabilities" was found.

### Power: beat the median of 13 with a paired-design calculation

The most common sample size at CHI 2014 was **12** ([Caine 2016](https://open.clemson.edu/computing_pubs/28); [dblp](https://dblp1.uni-trier.de/rec/conf/chi/Caine16.html)) [DOI 10.1145/2858036.2858498; median not visible] and the median group including people with disabilities across 506 CHI/ASSETS accessibility papers from 2010–2019 is **13** ([Mack et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.04271); [Makeability Lab](https://makeabilitylab.cs.washington.edu/project/accessibilityliteraturesurvey)) [peer-reviewed]. HCI methodologists urge estimation over significance testing ([Dragicevic 2016](https://www.lisn.upsaclay.fr/publications/fair-statistical-communication-in-hci/); [errata](https://aviz.fr/estimationreport); [HCI stats without p-values](https://aviz.fr/wiki/uploads/Research/HCI-stats-without-p-values.pdf)) [chapter DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-26633-6_13 from memory, unverified], and a Bonn seminar surveys effect-size under-reporting ([besec](https://besec.uni-bonn.de/course/ss24labpgsem/reporting_status_lab/)) [lead only]. For a within-subject design the primary contrast reduces to a paired-samples t-test, so power needs only an expected Cohen's d and the within-subject correlation, which strongly affects N ([PMC10439102](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10439102); [UCLA G*Power guide](https://stats.oarc.ucla.edu/gpower/power-analysis-for-paired-sample-t-test/)) [records]; defaults when no prior estimate exists are medium d, α .05 and power .80 in person or .90–.95 online ([UChicago HRI wiki](https://hri.cs.uchicago.edu/pages/wiki-entries/conducting-statistical-analyses-hri-data.html)) [wiki], with small-N and tool-support tutorials available ([UNE](https://rune.une.edu.au/web/handle/1959.11/54668); [PowerMeter](https://publica.fraunhofer.de/handle/publica/516658); [Arif](https://asarif.com/notes/EffectSamplePower.html)) [records]. Effect-size inputs will have to be extracted from the Wallace and Rello full texts or from a pilot. A study with **24–36 ADHD adults**, two to three times the field median, fully within-subject, with an a priori G*Power calculation (paired t, d from pilot, r ≈ 0.5) and preregistered mixed models would answer the standard "underpowered" critique; reviewers familiar with Dragicevic will expect means with 95% CIs rather than bare p-values. Preregistration is rare but valued: Cockburn, Gutwin and Dix made the case ([HARK No More](https://pure.cardiffmet.ac.uk/en/publications/hark-no-more-on-the-preregistration-of-chi-experiments/); [FORRT](https://forrt.org/curated_resources/hark-no-more-on-the-preregistration-of-c/)) [record] and only **1.11%** of papers across four CHI proceedings were preregistered ([Pang, Reinecke & Just 2022](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~rjust/publ/scaffolding_preregistration_chi_2022.pdf)) [DOI 10.1145/3491102.3517707]. OSF offers the general template with sample-size justification, the eight-question AsPredicted template and a social-psychology template ([OSF Help](https://help.osf.io/hc/en-us/articles/360019738794-Select-a-Registration-Template); [COS blog](https://www.cos.io/blog/choosing-preregistration-template-guide-for-researchers); [AsPredicted form](https://www.pomona.edu/sites/default/files/aspredicted-preregistration-template.docx)) [help pages]; releasing stimuli and code as Wallace and the ReadGen authors did is a visible differentiator. No CHI 2026 or ASSETS page formally rewards preregistration; the case rests on the methodology literature.

## Ethics review is achievable without an institution, and ADHD status is GDPR health data

Neither US nor EU law makes ethics review automatically mandatory for a privately funded behavioural study, but every target venue requires an ethics statement. CHI 2026 asks that studies with people have ethics review "under the requirements of the authors' research environment" and that authors explain that context to reviewers ([CHI 2026 Papers](https://chi2026.acm.org/authors/papers/)) [venue page]; indexed journals require an approval statement regardless of affiliation ([Editage](https://www.editage.com/insights/how-do-i-get-an-ethical-approval-without-being-affiliated-with-any-institute)) [secondary]; COPE has case guidance for studies with no committee ([COPE](https://publicationethics.org/guidance/case/studies-where-there-no-research-ethics-committee-or-where-committees-disagree-need-approval)) [guidance]. In the United States, the Common Rule binds institutions through a Federalwide Assurance only for federally conducted or supported research ([OHRP FWA terms](https://www.hhs.gov/ohrp/sites/default/files/ohrp/documents/terms092310rev.pdf); [CASRAI](https://www.casrai.org/dictionary/term/federalwide-assurance-fwa)) [government; OHRP's applicability text itself not retrieved]; the 2018 revision's exemption 46.104(d)(3) covers "benign behavioral interventions" with adults that are brief, harmless and not embarrassing, with limited IRB review if identifiable ([Pitt HRPO](https://www.hrpo.pitt.edu/node/903); [HHS slides](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/abcs-of-104-understanding-exemption-categories-slides.pdf); [CITI](https://about.citiprogram.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/Final-Rule-Material-Changes-to-Exempt-Determination-Process.pdf)) [government/secondary], which a reading study with ADHD adults plausibly fits. The practical route for an unaffiliated researcher is a commercial IRB such as Advarra or WCG, whose 2026 initial review fee is cited as **$5,900** though pricing is negotiated, or a university that reviews outside research, while some universities refuse unaffiliated researchers ([CASRAI cost guide](https://www.casrai.org/guides/irb-approval-cost); [SAGE community](https://researchmethodscommunity.sagepub.com/blog/independent-research-and-irb-reviews); [Fordham](https://www.fordham.edu/academics/research/institutional-review-board/guidelines-and-procedures/research-conducted-by-researchers-not-affiliated-with-fordham-university/); [UW–Madison fees](https://irb.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/sites/2/2024/06/IRBFeeschedule-5-2024.pdf); [UW–Madison industry policy](https://irb.wisc.edu/2024/06/20/update-to-considerations-for-industry-funded-studies-reviewed-locally/)) [secondary/institutional]; a budget of roughly US$2,000–6,000 for an exempt determination or expedited approval is the realistic expectation. In Europe, options are national independent ethics committees or a willing university board ([Editage](https://www.editage.com/insights/how-do-i-get-an-ethical-approval-without-being-affiliated-with-any-institute)), EU-funded work uses the Commission's ethics self-assessment ([UOW](https://ro.uow.edu.au/test2021/2129)), and requirements for low-risk studies "vary enormously" across countries ([PubMed 31159853](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31159853/)) [record]; no source listed specific EU commercial committees or fees.

Under GDPR an ADHD diagnosis is "data concerning health", whose processing Article 9(1) prohibits unless an Article 9(2) condition applies, such as explicit consent (9(2)(a)) or scientific research (9(2)(j)) with Article 89 safeguards including pseudonymisation ([gdpr-text.com Article 9](https://gdpr-text.com/en/read/article-9/); [Legiscope](https://www.legiscope.com/blog/health-data-article-9-gdpr.html)) [secondary text; EUR-Lex not fetched]. Health research needs both an Article 6 basis and an Article 9 exception, and the EDPB stresses that research-participation consent is distinct from GDPR consent as a legal basis and that explicit consent is not the only route ([EDPB 2021 via EJP RD](https://www.ejprarediseases.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/edpb_application-of-the-GDPR-on-health-research_2021.pdf); [A&L Goodbody](https://www.algoodbody.com/insights-publications/edpb-publishes-guidelines-on-processing-health-data-for-covid-19-research); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/edpb-guidelines-on-processing-personal-data-for-scientific-research-key-takeaways)) [guidance/secondary]; draft EDPB Guidelines 1/2026 on research were under consultation and stress participants' condition when judging free consent ([EFPIA response](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/consultation_feedback/1481/feedback_1481_efpia-response-to-the-edpb-consultation-on-guidelines-1_2026.pdf); [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/edpb-issues-draft-guidelines-for-use-of-9046036/)) [adoption status not confirmed]. The compliance set for an EU-based author is a documented Article 6 basis plus Article 9(2)(a) explicit consent for the ADHD-status field, separate consent clauses for participation and data processing, pseudonymised storage, a data-protection impact assessment if eye-tracking or large-scale health data are involved, and ethics review by a partner or national committee. Reviewers accept "exempt" or "reviewed by an independent IRB" when named explicitly.

## Gaps the Clear Read Hub study can fill

The literature leaves a cluster of openings that one well-designed study can address. There is no controlled group-level ADHD trial of chunking, headings, up-front summaries, bullet lists, progressive disclosure, reading rulers, reader mode, dark mode, keyword emphasis beyond three children, text simplification, automatic summarization or LLM-based restructuring, so a comparison of a structured or restructured document against a plain one with a diagnosed or screened ADHD sample and a control condition would be the first of its kind. No experiment has parametrically varied passage length in ADHD readers, and no eye-tracking study has examined multi-page or scrolling documents, so a design that manipulates section length within long documents and logs scrolling and re-reading would speak directly to the mind-wandering mechanism. No adult study uses real-world documents such as forms, contracts or technical documentation; every task so far is an experimental passage or a standardized test. No adaptive or personalised reading interface has been evaluated with ADHD participants and measured outcomes, although the personalisation literature predicts large individual variation and poor self-insight, so a within-user measurement loop would be novel. No readability corpus carries ADHD readers' judgements, so releasing passage-level comprehension, mind-wandering and preference data from ADHD and comparison readers would create the first such dataset. Measurement itself is a gap: no ADHD reading-interface study has reported NASA-TLX or SUS, the Israeli–North American divergence shows that gist and central-idea recall outperform short multiple-choice tests as sensitive outcomes, and medication status is almost never reported. Finally, the adult eye-tracking literature consists of two small Hebrew-language studies, so an English-language replication of the multi-pass re-reading signature, even with a webcam-grade tracker, would be a contribution in its own right.

## Conclusion

The evidence reorders the usual product intuition. The things ADHD reading apps advertise, special fonts, bolded word-starts, dark themes and speed-reading, either fail controlled tests or have never been tested with ADHD readers, while the interventions with mechanistic and empirical support are the unglamorous ones: letting the reader hear the text, prompting them to monitor their own understanding, structuring the page for scanning with an escape hatch to the original, and measuring rather than guessing what works for each person. Because ADHD readers' deficit lives in sustaining attention and retrieving the gist rather than in decoding, the right success metric for an adaptive document model is comprehension of central ideas at the reader's own pace with fewer attentional lapses, not words per minute, and the right architecture is a small set of user-controlled, measured adaptations rather than an "ADHD mode".

For the paper, the research base is simultaneously an opportunity and a hazard. A preregistered within-subject study of a few dozen screened adults with a comparison condition would exceed the methodological norms of a field whose median disability sample is 13 and whose two existing ADHD reading-interface studies are uncontrolled, and it could carry a released dataset no one else has. But the notes behind this report could not open full texts and carry many DOIs recalled from memory, inferred or in conflict; the grouped reference list below exists so that every citation in the manuscript can be checked against its primary record before the AI-assisted provenance of this review is disclosed, as the venues now require.
