## Key question 1: Which cognitive deficits in ADHD are implicated in reading comprehension, and what is the effect-size evidence?

### Takeaway
Working memory (WM) is the best-supported mechanism: child and adult meta-analyses put ADHD–control WM differences at medium size (d ≈ 0.4–1.0 in children, ≈ 0.5 in adults), and experimental dual-task work shows WM load selectively impairs comprehension in ADHD. Sustained attention and (spontaneous) mind-wandering are the next best-supported mechanisms, especially in adolescents and adults; inhibition is the weakest. Which executive function matters most is contested, and some studies find that vocabulary/word reading rather than executive function explains the gap.

### Cited Findings

**Working memory – meta-analytic effect sizes**
- Martinussen, Hayden, Hogg-Johnson & Tannock (2005), *J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry* 44(4):377–384 (*MA*, 26 studies, children). Effect sizes: spatial storage d = 0.85 (95% CI 0.62–1.08); spatial central executive d = 1.06 (0.72–1.39); verbal storage d = 0.47 (0.36–0.59); verbal central executive d = 0.43 (0.24–0.62). Deficits persisted after controlling for language and IQ. — [PubMed 15782085](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15782085/) **[DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1097/01.chi.0000153228.72591.73]**
- Willcutt, Doyle, Nigg, Faraone & Pennington (2005), "Validity of the executive function theory of ADHD: a meta-analytic review," *Biological Psychiatry* 57:1336–1346 (*MA*, 83 studies). Working memory pooled d = 0.55 (95% CI 0.44–0.65) per a summary table in a citing paper; EF deficits were medium (d ≈ 0.4–0.6) and not present in all individuals with ADHD. — [Colorado VIVO record](https://vivo.colorado.edu/display/pubid_52773); effect size via [PMC4264104 table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4264104/table/T4) **[DOI verified: 10.1016/j.biopsych.2005.02.006]**
- Alderson, Kasper, Hudec & Patros (2013), "ADHD and working memory in adults: a meta-analytic review," *Neuropsychology* 27(3):287–302 (*MA*, 38 adult studies). Verify before citing.
- Pievsky & McGrath (2018), "The neurocognitive profile of ADHD: a review of meta-analyses," *Archives of Clinical Neuropsychology* (*SR of MAs*). **Working memory – direct evidence for reading comprehension**
- Miller, Keenan, Betjemann, Willcutt, Pennington & Olson (2013), "Reading comprehension in children with ADHD: cognitive underpinnings of the centrality deficit," *J Abnormal Child Psychology* 41(3):473–483. Children with ADHD (mean age 9.78) vs word-reading-matched controls (mean age 9.89) retold a passage scored on 47 information units. ADHD group recalled more central than peripheral information, but its largest deficit relative to controls was on *central* information ("centrality deficit"). Among WM, inhibition, processing speed and IQ, **working memory accounted for the most unique variance** in central recall after controlling word reading. Sample sizes and effect sizes are in the full text, which could not be retrieved. — [PMC3561476](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3561476); [Colorado record](https://experts.colorado.edu/display/pubid_52750) **[DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1007/s10802-012-9686-8]**
- Kofler, Spiegel, Soto, Irwin, Wells & Austin (2019; online 2018), "Do working memory deficits underlie reading problems in ADHD?" *J Abnormal Child Psychology* 47(3):433–446. n = 78 children aged 8–13 (ADHD and non-ADHD); dual-task design with Bayesian modelling. Adding concurrent WM demands reduced comprehension in both groups but **more in ADHD**; effects robust to controls for decoding, sight-word vocabulary and comorbid reading disability. WM demands did not slow reading speed. Exploratory analyses: underdeveloped WM overlapped with 41% of the between-group comprehension difference and 85% of the reading-speed difference. — [FIU record](https://discovery.fiu.edu/display/pub200698); [PMC6301149](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6301149) **[DOI verified: 10.1007/s10802-018-0447-1]**
- Gropper & Tannock (2009), "A pilot study of working memory and academic achievement in college students with ADHD," *J Attention Disorders* 12(6):574–581. n = 16 ADHD vs 30 controls (university students). ADHD group significantly weaker on auditory-verbal WM tasks and one visual-spatial task; non-significant trend to lower self-reported GPA. Small pilot. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1087054708320390) **[DOI verified: 10.1177/1087054708320390]**

**Sustained attention**
- Stern & Shalev (2013), "The role of sustained attention and text's conditions in reading comprehension among adolescents with ADHD and without it," *Research in Developmental Disabilities* 34(1):431–439. Adolescents with/without ADHD read short passages in four conditions (standard vs spaced text; print vs screen) and performed a visual sustained-attention task. Significant group differences in comprehension and sustained attention; significant correlation between sustained attention and comprehension; significant interaction between presentation type, spacing and sustained-attention level. — [TAU lab publication list](https://attention.tau.ac.il/publications/published-articles/); summary at [behavioristbookclub](https://behavioristbookclub.com/aba-research/assessment-research/the-role-of-sustained-attention-and-display-medium-in-reading-comprehe/) **[DOI verified in a result: 10.1016/j.ridd.2012.08.021]** Sample size not seen.
- Segal (2023), "Sources of difficulties in reading comprehension in adults with ADHD," *Learning and Individual Differences*. n = 50 adults with ADHD vs 57 without. ADHD group scored lower on both sustained attention and reading comprehension; short-term memory and vocabulary did not differ; **only sustained attention predicted reading comprehension** in regression. — [Open University of Israel record](https://cris.openu.ac.il/en/publications/sources-of-difficulties-in-reading-comprehension-in-adults-with-a/) **[unverified: author initials, volume and DOI not seen]**
- Flory, Milich, Lorch, Hayden, Strange & Welsh (2006), "Online story comprehension among children with ADHD: which core deficits are involved?" *J Abnormal Child Psychology* 34(6):853–865. n = 49 ADHD vs 67 non-referred children aged 7–9, narrating a wordless book. ADHD narratives were not shorter but contained fewer causal-structure and goal-plan elements; **sustained attention accounted for the most variance** in these differences, ahead of inhibition and phonological processing. — [UKY record](https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/online-story-comprehension-among-children-with-adhd-which-core-de/) **[DOI verified: 10.1007/s10802-006-9070-7]** (Note: this is a narrative/story comprehension task, not print reading.)

**Mind-wandering**
- Bozhilova, Michelini, Kuntsi & Asherson (2018), "Mind wandering perspective on ADHD," *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 92:464–476 (narrative review). Proposes that altered default-mode-network deactivation and dysfunctional DMN–executive-control interaction produce excessive spontaneous mind-wandering (MW) that underpins ADHD symptoms; context regulation, sensory decoupling and salience thresholds are argued to be deficient in ADHD; MW proposed as a candidate endophenotype. - Mowlem, Agnew-Blais, Pingault & Asherson (2019), "Evaluating a scale of excessive mind wandering among males and females with and without ADHD from a population sample," *Scientific Reports*. — [UCL repository PDF](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10070531/7/Pingault_Evaluating%20a%20scale%20of%20excessive%20mind%20wandering%20among%20males%20and%20females%20with%20and%20without%20attention-deficit%20or%20hyperactivity%20disorder_VoR.pdf) **[DOI not seen]**
- Franklin, Mrazek, Anderson, Johnston, Smallwood, Kingstone & Schooler (2017; online 2014), "Tracking distraction: the relationship between mind-wandering, meta-awareness, and ADHD symptomatology," *J Attention Disorders* 21(6):475–486. Adult community sample n = 105 (71 female, mean age 23.1); lab tasks plus experience sampling. MW and "detrimental" MW positively associated with ADHD symptoms; meta-awareness of MW mediated the symptom → detrimental-MW link. (Not a reading-specific study.) — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/ref/10.1177/1087054714543494); [UCSB PDF](https://www.cmhp.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/2018-12/Franklin%20et%20al.%20%282014%29%20Tracking%20distraction.pdf) **[DOI verified: 10.1177/1087054714543494]**
- Bonifacci, Viroli, Vecchi, Tappi & Hu (2023; online 2022), "The relationship between mind wandering and reading comprehension: a meta-analysis," *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review* (*MA*, 25 studies, 73 correlations; general population, not ADHD). Pooled r = −0.21 between MW and comprehension; the correlation was stronger with online thought probes than with trait questionnaires (difference ≈ 0.30, with trait measures yielding a null correlation); more negative with increasing age; **text length, text difficulty, text type, interest and WM were not significant moderators.** — [PMC9971160](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9971160/); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.3758/s13423-022-02141-w) **[DOI verified: 10.3758/s13423-022-02141-w; author list after first author partly from memory]**

**Processing speed**
- McGrath et al. (2011), *J Child Psychol Psychiatry*: in a multiple-deficit SEM, processing speed was the only cognitive variable with unique relationships to both reading disability and ADHD (especially inattention) and primarily accounted for the reading–inattention correlation. Shanahan et al. ### Inferences
- The convergent picture for long documents: ADHD readers decode adequately but build a less connected mental model (centrality deficit, fewer inferences), with WM capacity and sustained attention as the mechanisms most consistently supported across ages. Effect sizes for the underlying cognitive deficits are medium (d ≈ 0.5), so group overlap is substantial and not every ADHD reader is affected.
- Mind-wandering appears to be the proximal, state-level mediator of the comprehension gap in adults (Brann & Sidi; Segal), while WM is the trait-level capacity explanation (Kofler; Miller). These are compatible: low WM capacity is a known predictor of MW in the general population.
- The Bonifacci meta-analysis finding that text length and difficulty do not moderate the MW–comprehension correlation (in general samples) should temper strong claims that length per se is the problem; see Q2.

### Gaps
- No meta-analysis of reading-comprehension effect sizes specifically in ADHD was found; Parks et al. (2022) is a scoping review without pooled effects.
- Kasper et al. (2012) could not be confirmed in this session.
- Exact n and effect sizes for Miller et al. (2013), Stern & Shalev (2013), Martinussen & Mackenzie (2015) and Segal (2023) were not retrievable.

## Key question 2: Does comprehension in ADHD readers degrade with passage length, time on task, or text structure? What experimental evidence exists?

### Takeaway
Direct experimental tests of *passage length* in ADHD readers are scarce; the strongest related evidence is (a) time-limit studies showing ADHD readers fail to finish long comprehension tests under standard time and benefit from extended time, (b) general-population evidence that longer text blocks increase unintentional mind-wandering, (c) layout evidence (spacing, screen vs print) interacting with sustained attention in ADHD adolescents, and (d) the "centrality deficit" showing the loss falls on main ideas. Claims that comprehension declines monotonically with length in ADHD rest on inference, not on a dedicated experiment I could find.

### Cited Findings
- Parks, Moreau et al. (2022), "The task matters: a scoping review on reading comprehension abilities in ADHD," *J Attention Disorders* 26(10):1304–1324 (*SR*, 34 studies). Evidence as a whole indicates impaired reading comprehension in ADHD; the magnitude varies considerably with task demands, with deficits most apparent on tasks requiring recall of central ideas. — [SAGE listing via citing paper](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/33851CEA7C1AC6193D088D4D5551ED3C/S1355617725101628a.pdf/reading-without-eye-movements-improving-reading-comprehension-in-young-adults-with-attention-deficithyperactivity-disorder-adhd.pdf) **[DOI verified: 10.1177/10870547211068047]**
- Brown, Reichel & Quinlan (2011), "Extended time improves reading comprehension test scores for adolescents with ADHD," *Open Journal of Psychiatry* 1(3). Retrospective chart review, n = 145 adolescents aged 13–18 with ADHD and no reading disorder. WM and processing-speed index scores were weaker than verbal comprehension; **under standard time 53% could not complete the reading comprehension test and only 42.8% scored within 1 SD of their verbal comprehension index; with extended time 77.9% did (p < 0.001).** Retrospective, uncontrolled, single clinic. — [SCIRP full text](https://file.scirp.org/Html/7817.html) **[DOI not seen]**
- Miller, Lewandowski & Antshel (2015; online 2013), "Effects of extended time for college students with and without ADHD," *J Attention Disorders* 19(8):678–686. n = 38 ADHD vs 38 matched controls; modified Nelson-Denny under standard, 1.5× and 2× time. Groups did **not** differ in items attempted or correct at any time condition; the ADHD group with extended time attempted and answered more than controls at standard time. — [SAGE TOC](https://journals.sagepub.com/toc/jada/19/8); [MetaTOC](https://metatoc.com/papers/2191-effects-of-extended-time-for-college-students-with-and-without-adhd) **[DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1177/1087054713483308]** Contradicts Brown et al. (2011) on whether ADHD readers are differentially time-limited; different populations (clinic adolescents vs college students).
- Lewandowski, Cohen & Lovett (2013), "Effects of extended time allotments on reading comprehension performance of college students with and without learning disabilities," *J Psychoeducational Assessment*. Typical students benefited more from 50% and 100% extra time than LD students, so extended time is not disability-specific. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0734282912462693) **[DOI verified: 10.1177/0734282912462693]**
- Ghelani, Sidhu, Jain & Tannock (2004), "Reading comprehension and reading related abilities in adolescents with reading disabilities and ADHD," *Dyslexia* 10(4):364–384. Four groups (RD, ADHD, ADHD+RD, controls). ADHD-only adolescents had adequate single-word reading but **subtle difficulties on text reading rate and accuracy and on silent reading comprehension**, with scores still in the average range; the comorbid group had RD-like word-reading and rate problems and comprehension problems only on silent reading. — [Scholarhub listing](https://scholarhub.vn/publisher/Dyslexia/d5cb32c5-e663-4e98-bfd5-7fc6cd0f778d) **[DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1002/dys.285; n not seen]**
- Stern & Shalev (2013) (details in Q1): text spacing and display medium (print vs screen) interacted with sustained-attention level in predicting comprehension in adolescents with/without ADHD; a secondary summary adds that high-sustained-attention readers answered more questions correctly and read faster than average/low. — [TAU list](https://attention.tau.ac.il/publications/published-articles/); summary in [Children 2024 citing paper](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9067/11/10/1245) **[DOI verified]**
- Miller et al. (2013) centrality deficit (Q1): the loss is concentrated on central/main-idea content, implying that summarising or recalling the gist of a long document is specifically vulnerable. **[DOI recalled, unverified]**
- Yeari, Vakil, Schifer & Schiff (2019), "The origin of the centrality deficit in individuals with ADHD," *J Clinical and Experimental Neuropsychology*. n = 28 adults with ADHD vs 27 controls, eye tracking. ADHD adults recalled fewer central ideas but recognised them and rated their importance as well as controls → the deficit lies in **retrieval of central ideas from long-term memory** and in establishing fewer connections between ideas during reading. — [Bar-Ilan record](https://education.biu.ac.il/en/node/9754); [IUCC record](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/the-origin-of-the-centrality-deficit-in-individuals-with-attentio/) **[DOI not seen]**
- Yeari & Lavie (2021), think-aloud study in adolescents, *Learning Disabilities Research & Practice*: ADHD group produced fewer deep-processing responses and reinstated fewer prior (especially central) ideas while reading. — [IUCC record](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/the-role-of-surface-text-processing-in-centrality-deficit-and-poo/) **[unverified details]**
- Avramovich (2015, Bar-Ilan master's thesis): readers with ADHD generated fewer bridging and elaborative inferences; accuracy on questions did not differ but responses were significantly slower. — [thesis PDF](https://education.biu.ac.il/files/education/shared/avramovichadi.pdf) **[thesis, not peer-reviewed]**
- Forrin, Mills, D'Mello, Risko, Smilek & Seli (2021), "TL;DR: longer sections of text increase rates of unintentional mind-wandering," *J Experimental Education* 89(2):278–290. n = 80 undergraduates (general population) reading ~500-word Wikipedia passages at 9th-grade level in one-sentence vs 2–6-sentence screens with thought probes. **Unintentional MW 19% (short sections) vs 24% (long sections); intentional MW unaffected.** No direct comprehension effect reported in the abstract. — [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10284384); [Colorado record](https://experts.colorado.edu/display/pubid_285372) **[DOI not seen]**
- Hurst (2020), University of Waterloo MA thesis, "On the relationships between mind-wandering, ADHD, engagingness, and effort": higher ADHD symptoms predicted more inattention during *unengaging* textbook passages, more so when they came second (time on task), but **no effect of ADHD symptoms on comprehension**. — [UWSpace](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/b6000f50-3ae8-4613-a6eb-19002513f983) **[thesis, not peer-reviewed]**
- Bonifacci et al. (2023) meta-analysis (Q1): text length and difficulty were **not** significant moderators of the MW–comprehension relation in general samples. **[DOI verified]**
- Moussaoui, Siddiqi, Cheung & Niemeier (2025), "Reading without eye movements: improving reading comprehension in young adults with ADHD," *J International Neuropsychological Society*. Undergraduates with/without ADHD read short paragraphs as normal text, paced word-by-word in place, or RSVP (words at screen centre). ADHD group comprehension improved under RSVP by almost 13% relative to controls, who did worse with RSVP; authors conclude eye movements may interfere with comprehension in ADHD. Sample size not seen. — [Cambridge Core reader](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/33851CEA7C1AC6193D088D4D5551ED3C/core-reader) **[DOI inferred from Cambridge PII, unverified: 10.1017/S1355617725101628]** Single study; note general-population work finds RSVP *harms* comprehension (e.g., trailing-mask study in *Psychological Science*, [APS summary](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/were-only-human/read-this-blog-post-in-less-than-a-minute.html)).
- Brock (1996) [co-author "Knapp" **not confirmed** by any search result], "Reading comprehension abilities of children with ADHD," pp. 173–185 (journal not shown in the record; the requester's "Journal of Attention Disorders" attribution is plausible but unverified). n = 21 ADHD vs 21 matched intermediate-grade children. ADHD group had lower comprehension scores; hypothesised interaction between group and comprehension level, and comprehension-monitoring differences, were **not** supported. — [CSUS record](https://scholars.csus.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Reading-comprehension-abilities-of-children-with/99257876658401671) **[unverified]**

### Inferences
- The most defensible accessibility claim is not "ADHD readers lose comprehension as a function of word count" but: (1) ADHD readers take longer (Ghelani; Stern 2024 eye tracking; Brown), so fixed time budgets convert into incomplete reading; (2) longer unbroken sections increase unintentional MW even in typical readers (Forrin) and MW mediates the ADHD comprehension gap (Brann & Sidi); (3) losses fall disproportionately on central ideas (Miller; Yeari), which is what summaries and gist questions probe. Chunking, spacing and scaffolded self-monitoring are therefore the interventions with the clearest mechanistic rationale.
- Text-structure manipulations (spacing, medium) interact with sustained attention (Stern & Shalev 2013), supporting layout-level accessibility interventions, but that is one adolescent study.

### Gaps
- I found **no peer-reviewed experiment that parametrically varied passage length in a diagnosed ADHD sample** and measured comprehension; the search budget ran out before a targeted query on this ("time on task / vigilance decrement in reading") could run.
- "Hebrew and Hoglund" as cited by the requester: **[could not identify]** no paper with an author named Hoglund on ADHD reading comprehension surfaced. The requester may be thinking of the Hebrew-language studies by Stern & Shalev (Tel Aviv) or Yeari (Bar-Ilan). Recommend they re-check the reference.
- A 2006 study reporting no ADHD–control difference on literal/inferential comprehension but worse story recall and ordering (apparently in *Advances in Learning and Behavioral Disabilities* vol. 19, [Emerald](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1016/S0735-004X(06)19009-6)) could not be attributed to specific authors in this session.

## Key question 3: Prevalence of ADHD–dyslexia comorbidity and how studies isolate ADHD-specific reading effects

### Takeaway
Reviews converge on 25–40% bidirectional comorbidity, driven partly by shared genetic influence and a shared processing-speed deficit (multiple-deficit model). ADHD-specific reading effects are isolated by (a) four-group designs (ADHD, RD, ADHD+RD, control), (b) matching or statistically controlling word reading/decoding, (c) excluding RD, and (d) dual-task designs that control decoding and comorbid RD.

### Cited Findings
- Boada, Willcutt & Pennington (2012), "Understanding the comorbidity between dyslexia and ADHD," *Topics in Language Disorders* 32(3):264–284. Dyslexia and ADHD co-occur more than chance; **25–40% of children with one disorder meet criteria for the other**; both reframed under a multiple-deficit model. — [Colorado record](https://experts.colorado.edu/display/pubid_53929); [PDF](https://alliedhealth.ceconnection.com/files/UnderstandingtheComorbidityBetweenDyslexiaandAttentionDeficitHyperactivityDisorder-1352212337316.pdf) **[DOI verified: 10.1097/TLD.0b013e31826203ac]**
- Germanò, Gagliano & Curatolo (2010), "Comorbidity of ADHD and dyslexia," *Developmental Neuropsychology* 35(5):475–493. Comorbidity is frequent; comorbid children show an additive neuropsychological profile and worse academic/behavioural outcome; RD may mark a more severely affected ADHD subgroup. — [Unime record](https://iris.unime.it/handle/11570/1907750) **[DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1080/87565641.2010.494748]**
- Willcutt & Pennington (2000), "Comorbidity of reading disability and ADHD: differences by gender and subtype," *J Learning Disabilities* 33(2):179–191. Community twin sample, n = 494 with RD vs 373 without. RD children more likely to meet ADHD criteria; association **stronger for inattention than hyperactivity-impulsivity**; hyperactivity association only in boys. — [Scholarhub listing](https://scholarhub.vn/publication/Comorbidity-of-Reading-Disability-and-Attention-Deficit-Hyperactivity-Disorder/63fc18e8-f8b3-41da-be2d-7bba2c68575e) **[DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1177/002221940003300206]**
- Willcutt, Pennington & DeFries (2000), "Twin study of the etiology of comorbidity between RD and ADHD," *Am J Medical Genetics* 96:293–301: comorbidity attributable substantially to common genetic influences, especially for inattention. — [Colorado record](https://experts.colorado.edu/display/pubid_52784) **[DOI not seen]**
- Willcutt, Betjemann, McGrath, Chhabildas, Olson, DeFries & Pennington (2010), "Etiology and neuropsychology of comorbidity between RD and ADHD: the case for multiple-deficit models," *Cortex* 46(10). Colorado Learning Disabilities Research Center twin sample; both disorders associated with slow processing speed; significant genetic correlation between RD and ADHD. — [ScienceDaily summary](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2010/12/101208125805.htm); [PMC3079018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3079018) **[DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1016/j.cortex.2010.06.009]**
- McGrath & Stoodley (2019), "Are there shared neural correlates between dyslexia and ADHD? A meta-analysis of VBM studies," *J Neurodevelopmental Disorders* (*MA*, 15 dyslexia + 22 ADHD studies). Largely distinct grey-matter differences; no significant overlap at conservative threshold; one overlapping region (right caudate) at lenient threshold. — [PubMed 31752659](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31752659/) **[DOI not seen]**
- Laasonen et al. (2010), *J Learning Disabilities*: 110 adults with dyslexia, ADHD or neither; ADHD adults were the least affected on phonological processing, reading and spelling, and their deficits became non-significant after controlling IQ. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022219409335216) **[DOI verified: 10.1177/0022219409335216]**
- Deans, O'Laughlin, Brubaker, Gay & Krug (2010) (Q4) cites comorbidity estimates of 10–45%. — [SCIRP](https://file.scirp.org/Html/2920.html)
- Stark et al. (2022), South African retrospective study of 847 learners aged 8–18 on ADHD/dyslexia co-occurrence (rate not captured). — [UP repository](https://repository.up.ac.za/handle/2263/89687) **[unverified]**

**How ADHD-specific effects are isolated (design examples)**
- Four-group factorial design (ADHD, RD, ADHD+RD, control): Ghelani et al. (2004); Deans et al. (2010). — see above.
- Matching controls on word reading and statistically controlling word reading before testing cognitive predictors: Miller et al. (2013). — [PMC3561476](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3561476)
- Excluding reading disorder from the ADHD sample: Brown et al. (2011) (no specific reading disorder); Molina et al. (2020) ("pure" non-medicated ADHD). — [SCIRP](https://file.scirp.org/Html/7817.html); [Molina record](https://www.ibsgranada.es/en/articulos/children-with-attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-show-an-altered-eye-movement-pattern-during-reading/)
- Controlling decoding, sight-word vocabulary and comorbid RD within a dual-task design: Kofler et al. (2019). **[DOI verified]**
- Dimensional (symptom-rating) designs in community samples that model word reading as a mediator: Cain & Bignell (2014).

### Inferences
- Because 25–40% of ADHD samples carry RD, any ADHD reading study that does not screen or control decoding risks attributing dyslexic decoding effects to attention. Reviewers of an HCI paper will expect the cited ADHD effects to come from designs that control decoding (Miller; Kofler; Ghelani; Brown).
- The RD–inattention (not hyperactivity) link means screening by inattentive symptoms (e.g., ASRS inattention items) is the more relevant axis for reading research.

### Gaps
- No pooled-prevalence meta-analysis of ADHD–dyslexia comorbidity was found; the 25–40% figure comes from narrative reviews.
- Adult-specific comorbidity prevalence figures were not found; Laasonen (2010) is the only adult three-group study located.

## Key question 4: What do eye-tracking studies show about reading in ADHD?

### Takeaway
Children with ADHD (even without RD) show more fixations, more regressions and slower reading on oral reading tasks; adults with ADHD show longer reading times and markedly more re-reading of words (a proposed marker of "inattentive reading"), with first-pass fixations less sensitive to word length. Eye movements do not reliably discriminate ADHD from RD. The adult literature is very thin (two small Israeli studies and one preregistered German study with results pending).

### Cited Findings
- Molina, Redondo et al. (2020), "Children with ADHD show an altered eye movement pattern during reading," *Optometry and Vision Science* 97(4):265–274. n = 21 non-medicated children with "pure" ADHD vs 20 age-matched controls; oral reading of a standardized text with Visagraph. ADHD: **more fixations, more regressions, more return-sweep saccades, more fixation/regression anomalies, longer reading time, lower reading rate and grade-level equivalent**; authors state the pattern cannot be attributed to comorbidity. — [IBS Granada record](https://www.ibsgranada.es/en/articulos/children-with-attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-show-an-altered-eye-movement-pattern-during-reading/); [UGR blog](https://blogs.ugr.es/claro-group/?p=507) **[DOI verified: 10.1097/OPX.0000000000001498]** (one listing gives second author as "Round, B." — likely an indexing error for "Redondo").
- Deans, O'Laughlin, Brubaker, Gay & Krug (2010), "Use of eye movement tracking in the differential diagnosis of ADHD and reading disability," *Psychology* 1:238–246. n = 27 ADHD, 20 RD, 30 controls, ages 6–12. RD children slower, longer fixations, more atypical movements; ADHD group also more atypical movements than controls; **only total reading time differed between ADHD and RD**; discriminant analysis (reading time + proportion left-to-right saccades) classified **< 60%** correctly. — [SCIRP](https://file.scirp.org/Html/2920.html); [ISU thesis](https://scholars.indianastate.edu/etds/1307) **[DOI not seen; low-tier open-access publisher]**
- Stern, Kolodny, Tsafrir, Cohen & Shalev (2024), "Unique patterns of eye movements characterizing inattentive reading in ADHD," *J Attention Disorders* 28(6):1008–1016. Eye movements during uninterrupted text reading in adults with ADHD vs typically developed adults. **Significantly longer reading time in ADHD; words re-read more than twice were much more frequent in ADHD; first-pass reading of those words was less sensitive to word length** (interpreted as less meaningful reading). Authors propose high re-read rate as a correlate of inattentive reading. Sample size not seen. — [IUCC record](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/unique-patterns-of-eye-movements-characterizing-inattentive-readi/); [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/ref/10.1177/10870547231223728) **[DOI verified: 10.1177/10870547231223728]**
- Yeari, Vakil, Schifer & Schiff (2019) (Q2): eye tracking in 28 ADHD adults vs 27 controls; central-idea deficit located in retrieval, not in online noticing/importance rating. — [Bar-Ilan record](https://education.biu.ac.il/en/node/9754) **[DOI not seen]**
- Bar-Ilan group also lists "The role of planning in reading comprehension by adolescents with ADHD: evidence from eye-movements" (details not retrieved). — [record](https://education.biu.ac.il/en/node/12060) **[unverified]**
- Preregistered German study (Kullik et al., TeaP 2026 abstract): n = 100 adults (50 with clinically verified ADHD) reading garden-path sentences; prediction of more regressions in ADHD; **results not yet reported**. — [TeaP abstract](https://coms.events/teap26/data/abstracts/en/abstract_0268_0494_0565.html) **[conference abstract; no results]**
- Krejtz et al. (2015): adults with ADHD fixated salient cues like controls but showed less structured, more "chaotic" scan patterns (non-reading task; cited in a Frontiers in Communication 2024 paper). — [Frontiers PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2024.1482105/pdf) **[secondary citation only]**
- Moussaoui et al. (2025) (Q2): removing the need for eye movements (RSVP) improved comprehension in ADHD undergraduates by ~13% relative to controls. **[DOI inferred, unverified]**
- Eye-movement markers of mind wandering during reading have been meta-analysed (Turku group, general population): longer/more variable fixations and fewer regressions characterise MW episodes. — [UTU record](https://www.utupub.fi/items/94d7c802-6f7d-458d-9c32-05d24688a58d) **[authors/year/DOI not captured]**

### Inferences
- The re-reading signature in adults (Stern et al. 2024) maps directly onto a plausible accessibility metric: time spent re-reading and number of multi-pass words could serve as an unobtrusive indicator of attentional lapse during document reading.
- Because eye movements overlap heavily between ADHD and RD (Deans), eye-tracking cannot be used to infer diagnosis, only to characterise reading behaviour.

### Gaps
- Only two adult ADHD reading eye-tracking studies with results were found (Stern 2024; Yeari 2019), both small and from Israeli labs using Hebrew texts; generalisation to English and to long documents is untested.
- No eye-tracking study of ADHD readers on multi-page or scrolling documents was found.

## Key question 5: Which standardized measures and screening tools are used for adults with ADHD in reading research?

### Takeaway
Adult reading studies typically verify diagnosis by clinical history and confirm current symptoms with the ASRS (WHO 6-item screener; DSM-IV 2005 version or DSM-5 2017 version), the CAARS (self/observer rating), and/or the DIVA semi-structured interview (DIVA 2.0 / DIVA-5); comprehension is most often measured with the Nelson-Denny Reading Test in North American college samples, plus a sustained-attention task (CPT-type).

### Cited Findings
- Kessler, Adler, Ames, Demler, Faraone, Hiripi, Howes, Jin, Secnik, Spencer, Ustun & Walters (2005), "The World Health Organization Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS): a short screening scale for use in the general population," *Psychological Medicine* 35(2):245–256. 18 items keyed to DSM-IV Criterion A; 6-item screener selected by stepwise logistic regression against clinical diagnoses. — [PubMed 15841682](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15841682/) **[DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1017/S0033291704002892; author list partly from memory]**
- Adler et al. (2006), ASRS v1.1 pilot validation: Cronbach's α = 0.88 (patient) and 0.89 (rater). — [Scribd copy of Adler 2006](https://www.scribd.com/document/694650102/adler2006) **[low-quality host; verify]**
- Ustun, Adler, Rudin, Faraone, Spencer, Berglund, Gruber & Kessler (2017), "The WHO Adult ADHD Self-Report Screening Scale for DSM-5," *JAMA Psychiatry* 74(5):520–527. Six items derived by a risk-calibrated supersparse linear integer model. General population (weighted to 8.2% prevalence): sensitivity 91.4%, specificity 96.0%, AUC 0.94, PPV 67.3%; NYU clinical sample (57.7% prevalence): sensitivity 91.9%, specificity 74.0%, AUC 0.83, PPV 82.8%. — [Harvard HCP record](https://hcp.hms.harvard.edu/publication/world-health-organization-adult-attention-deficithyperactivity-disorder-self-report); [Duke record](https://scholars.duke.edu/individual/pub1290227) **[DOI recalled, unverified: 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2017.0298]** A secondary site reports Kessler recommends unweighted scoring because DSM-5 weighted scoring over-estimates prevalence — unverified.
- Conners Adult ADHD Rating Scales (CAARS; Conners, Erhardt & Sparrow, 1999, MHS) — described in validation papers as the international standard questionnaire. German validation (Christiansen et al., *European Psychiatry*): n = 850 community controls, six-factor solution; α = .74–.95; test-retest .85–.92 for CAARS-S. Catalan short forms: four-factor model adequate; test-retest r = .80 (self) and .73 (observer). CAARS 2 (2023) manual exists. — [Bern repository](https://boris.unibe.ch/5789/); [Cambridge](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-psychiatry/article/german-validation-of-the-conners-adult-adhd-rating-scalesselfreport-caarss-i-factor-structure-and-normative-data/9CF64E95C5C7FD8080E0A8264441EBF5); [CAARS 2 manual](https://manuals.mhs.com/caars2/references/) **[original 1999 manual citation from memory]**
- DIVA 2.0 (Kooij & Francken, 2010) and DIVA-5 (Kooij, Francken, Bron & Wynchank, 2019; DSM-5 criteria, developed with Asherson and Ramos-Quiroga). DIVA 2.0 criterion/concurrent validity vs CAADID: Ramos-Quiroga et al., *European Psychiatry* (Spanish sample). — [Cambridge](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/european-psychiatry/article/criteria-and-concurrent-validity-of-diva-20-a-semistructured-diagnostic-interview-for-adult-adhd/7063C91BB5E1713FBB08D1A42CF0DE35) **[DOI not seen]**
- DIVA-5 Korean validation (Hong et al., 2020, *Neuropsychiatric Disease and Treatment*): n = 279 across nine outpatient clinics; diagnostic accuracy 92%, sensitivity 91.30%, specificity 93.62% against psychiatrist diagnosis. — [PubMed 33116536](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33116536/); [PMC7567566](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7567566) **[DOI not seen]** Persian DIVA-5 study (n = 120): 38% met criteria on DIVA-5 vs 55% on SCID-5. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/1087054720930816) **[DOI verified: 10.1177/1087054720930816]**
- Mind Excessively Wandering Scale (MEWS; Mowlem et al., 2019/2016) — 12-item measure specific to spontaneous MW in ADHD, usable as a mechanism measure in reading studies. — [Greenwich record](https://gala.gre.ac.uk/id/eprint/32762/)
- Reading outcome measures used in adult ADHD studies: Nelson-Denny Reading Test (Miller, Lewandowski & Antshel 2015; Lewandowski et al. 2013; a Syracuse dissertation found no ADHD–control differences on decoding, speed, vocabulary or comprehension but differences in test anxiety). — [SAGE TOC](https://journals.sagepub.com/toc/jada/19/8); [Syracuse SURFACE](https://surface.syr.edu/psy_etd/2) Passage-recall scored on information units (Miller 2013; Yeari 2019); BRIEF-A self-report EF plus performance tasks (Markovich 2022 conference study, n = 140 undergraduates). — [IATED](https://library.iated.org/view/MARKOVICH2022EXE)

### Inferences
- For an HCI/accessibility study recruiting adults, the ASRS-5 (2017) is the defensible screener (short, published operating characteristics), with CAARS or DIVA-5 for confirmation if diagnosis-level claims are needed; MEWS adds a mechanism-specific measure for mind-wandering. Note that specificity of ASRS-5 drops substantially in high-prevalence (self-selected) samples.

### Gaps
- No original English-language DIVA-5 validation paper was found; validations located are Korean, Persian and (reportedly) Italian.
- Could not retrieve the original CAARS (1999) manual citation or DOI.

## Key question 6: What is known about adults specifically?

### Takeaway
Adult evidence is sparse and small-sample but consistent in direction: adults with ADHD decode normally, read more slowly with more re-reading, comprehend long expository text less well, recall fewer central ideas, and the gap is statistically explained by sustained attention and state mind-wandering rather than vocabulary or short-term memory. Working-memory deficits persist into adulthood at moderate effect size. Interventions with evidence in adults: extended time (contested), metacognitive scaffolding (one RCT-style study), and RSVP presentation (one study).

### Cited Findings
- Alderson et al. (2013) adult WM meta-analysis: 38 studies, moderate effects (secondary figures 0.55 verbal / 0.49 visuospatial). **[DOI recalled, unverified]** — [record](https://vufind.lboro.ac.uk/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_miscellaneous_1353987286)
- Segal (2023): 50 ADHD vs 57 control adults; only sustained attention predicted comprehension. **[unverified details]** — [record](https://cris.openu.ac.il/en/publications/sources-of-difficulties-in-reading-comprehension-in-adults-with-a/)
- Brann & Sidi (2025): 210 adults; scaffolding eliminated the ADHD comprehension gap; state MW mediated. **[DOI not seen]** — [record](https://cris.openu.ac.il/en/publications/metacognitive-scaffolding-for-digital-reading-and-mind-wandering-/)
- Stern et al. (2024): adult ADHD longer reading time and more multi-pass re-reading. **[DOI verified]** — [record](https://cris.iucc.ac.il/en/publications/unique-patterns-of-eye-movements-characterizing-inattentive-readi/)
- Yeari et al. (2019): 28 ADHD adults; centrality deficit located in retrieval. **[DOI not seen]** — [record](https://education.biu.ac.il/en/node/9754)
- Miller, Lewandowski & Antshel (2015): 38 vs 38 college students; no group differences on Nelson-Denny at any time limit. **[DOI recalled, unverified]** — [TOC](https://journals.sagepub.com/toc/jada/19/8)
- Gropper & Tannock (2009): 16 vs 30 college students; verbal WM weaknesses. **[DOI verified]** — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1087054708320390)
- Laasonen et al. (2010): 110 adults; ADHD adults least impaired on reading/phonology, non-significant after IQ control. **[DOI verified]** — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022219409335216)
- Franklin et al. (2017): 105 adults; ADHD symptoms ↔ MW and detrimental MW; meta-awareness mediates. **[DOI verified]** — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/ref/10.1177/1087054714543494)
- Moussaoui et al. (2025): RSVP benefit ~13% in ADHD undergraduates. **[DOI inferred]** — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/33851CEA7C1AC6193D088D4D5551ED3C/core-reader)
- Small Portuguese study (2022, *Diacrítica*): 15 undergraduates with ADHD vs 15 without; reduced WM capacity associated with poorer comprehension. — [UMinho](https://revistas.uminho.pt/index.php/diacritica/article/view/4779) **[small, regional journal]**
- Markovich (2022, INTED conference): 140 undergraduates; significant effects of ADHD diagnosis and comprehension proficiency on BRIEF-A and performance EF measures. — [IATED](https://library.iated.org/view/MARKOVICH2022EXE) **[conference paper]**
- A study of 53 adults with ADHD (mostly university students/applicants) reports that comprehension and fluency difficulties are common and may relate to EF deficits. — [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/works/9074309) **[authors/year not captured; unverified]**
- Samuelsson, Lundberg & Herkner (2004), "ADHD and reading disability in male adults: is there a connection?" *J Learning Disabilities* 37(2):155–168 (as I recall). Only an author-page listing was found; an automated summary states adult men with ADHD did not differ in phonological processing or decoding but performed poorly on comprehension. **[unverified: journal/year/DOI/sample not confirmed by any primary record; treat as unconfirmed]** — [research.com author page](https://research.com/u/stefan-samuelsson); [Consensus summary](https://consensus.app/questions/adhd-men/)
- Students' lived experience of ADHD at university (meta-ethnography, 2022) documents reading-load difficulty qualitatively. — [ECU repository](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworks2022-2026/8906) **[not reading-specific]**

### Inferences
- The adult evidence base is dominated by (a) North American college samples with mixed/null comprehension findings on standardized timed tests and (b) Israeli lab samples with consistent deficits on long expository text with recall/central-idea measures. The divergence is itself informative: standardized multiple-choice tests of short passages may be insensitive to the ADHD comprehension problem, which emerges with long text, recall and gist (consistent with Parks et al. 2022, "the task matters").

### Gaps
- No adult meta-analysis of reading comprehension in ADHD exists to my knowledge; none was found.
- Medication status is rarely reported in the adult reading studies located; Molina (2020) is the only study found that explicitly used non-medicated participants.
- No study of adults with ADHD reading real-world documents (forms, contracts, technical documentation) was found; all tasks were experimental passages or standardized tests.
